\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

\paragraph{The Vietnamese syllable on the page.}
A written Vietnamese syllable is onset + rime + tone, the rime being an optional glide, a nucleus and an optional coda \citep{thompson-1965-vietnamese,doan-1977-nguam,nguyen-1997-vietnamese}. There are six tones: \vi{ngang} (unmarked), \vi{huyền}, \vi{sắc}, \vi{hỏi}, \vi{ngã} and \vi{nặng} \citep{kirby-2011-vietnamese,pham-2003-vietnamese-tone}. Spelling depends on the neighbours (/k/ is \vi{k} before \vi{e ê i}, \vi{q} before the glide, \vi{c} elsewhere), and stop-final syllables take only \vi{sắc} or \vi{nặng}. Two conventions place the tone mark, differing only on the open rimes \vi{oa}, \vi{oe}, \vi{uy} after an onset other than \vi{qu}: the older marks the first letter (\vi{hòa}, \vi{thủy}), the newer the second (\vi{hoà}, \vi{thuỷ}). Exactly 69 of the 6,611 syllables of the standard spelling list differ \citep{hunspell-vi}; both conventions are in everyday use, and in XCOPA-vi 60 of the 62 tokens whose placement differs follow the older one (\placeholder{corpus count on two registers before Gate~1 fixes the baseline; DESIGN\_DECISIONS 6.3}). Appendix~\ref{app:rules} tabulates every rule as the engine implements it.

\paragraph{Three encodings of the same letters.}
A Vietnamese letter carries up to two diacritics, a quality mark (\vi{ă â ê ô ơ ư}) and a tone mark. Unicode stores the same text in NFC, one code point per letter, or in NFD, base letter plus combining marks in canonical order, which puts the tone mark \emph{before} the quality mark in \vi{ộ ậ ặ ệ}; a partially composed form (PC) from Windows-1258 survives in legacy text. A tokenizer that normalizes its input to NFC erases the difference, one that passes the input through receives a different token sequence for the same word, and some tokenizer paths \emph{corrupt} combining marks by deleting them (Section~\ref{sec:setup}).

\paragraph{Nói lái.}
In \vi{nói lái}, the Vietnamese spoonerism, two syllables exchange components so that a new, often humorous, phrase appears. Writing a syllable as $(O, R, T)$, a variant is a subset of $\{O, R, T\}$ exchanged between the two positions. The empty subset is the identity and the full one plain reversal; the six others are the six types described in popular and press sources \citep{le-ho-1990-thu-choi-chu,nguyen-van-hiep-noi-lai}: \var{1} swaps the rimes (\vi{mèo cái} `female cat' $\rightarrow$ \vi{mài kéo} `sharpen scissors'); \var{2} swaps onset and rime, so that the syllables trade places while the tones stay (\vi{đầu tiên} `first' $\rightarrow$ \vi{tiền đâu} `where's the money?'); \var{3} swaps the tones (\vi{bí mật} `secret' $\rightarrow$ \vi{bị mất} `got lost'); \var{4} swaps rime and tone (\vi{bí mật} $\rightarrow$ \vi{bật mí} `reveal'); \var{5} swaps the onsets and \var{6} onsets and tones. The eight subsets form the group $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^3$, and reading an output in the other order pairs the variants: reverse(\var{1}) = \var{6}, reverse(\var{2}) = \var{3}, reverse(\var{4}) = \var{5}. We generate items for \var{1}--\var{4} and keep \var{5} and \var{6} in the \task{T2} gold and the \task{T3} twins. The sources attach regional labels to the variants inconsistently, so our codes are region-neutral and validators are region-tagged instead. Some attested examples, as in the poetry of Hồ Xuân Hương, have vulgar readings, which the release flags (see the Ethical Considerations section).
