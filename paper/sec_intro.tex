\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Written Vietnamese is a Latin-alphabet script in which every syllable is a separate word and its three parts are visible on the page: an onset consonant, a rime (a vowel sequence with an optional final consonant) and one of six tones, marked by a diacritic or by its absence. A folk art takes syllables apart at exactly these seams. \vi{Nói lái} swaps the rimes, the tones or both between two syllables of a phrase: \vi{mèo cái} `female cat' becomes \vi{mài kéo} `sharpen scissors' and \vi{bí mật} `secret' becomes \vi{bị mất} `got lost'. The transformation lives entirely below the word, and the spelling of the result follows rules that depend on the new neighbours (\vi{eo} after /k/ turns \vi{c} into \vi{k}).

Subword tokenizers see none of this. They split syllables where merge statistics dictate, and text in a different but equally valid Unicode encoding is split differently again. Figure~\ref{fig:tokens} shows the Gemma~3 tokenizer on the example: in the precomposed (NFC) encoding the output syllable \vi{mài} is cut between onset and rime, and in the combining (NFD) encoding the tone mark of \vi{mèo} becomes a token of its own. Over the 6,595 standard syllables it splits 70\% under NFC, half of them exactly at the onset--rime seam, and spends one extra token per syllable under NFD, isolating the tone mark in 42\% of syllables.\footnote{From the tokenizer audit released with our code (\anonrepo); the tokenizer does not normalize NFD to NFC, so the two encodings reach the model as different token sequences (Section~\ref{sec:setup}).} We ask: \emph{can a subword LLM reach the onset, rime and tone inside a tonal syllable it never receives as a unit, and does failure grow with how badly the tokens cut across those units?}

Nói lái is our instrument, not our headline. Because its variants are permutations of syllable components and Vietnamese spelling is rule-governed, a small rule engine generates transformation, decoding and validity items without limit, verifies every gold answer and scores every output by parsing it; no LLM judge is needed. Because Vietnamese writes all three units in the string, a model that cannot manipulate them fails on access to spelled units, not on recall of unspelled pronunciations, the confound that burdens the same question in Chinese or Filipino (Section~\ref{sec:related}). The same engine re-encodes any Vietnamese text between canonically equivalent Unicode forms, and between the two tone-mark placement conventions, with meaning and information fixed, a training-free intervention on deployed tokenizers; and the two smallest Gemma~3 models let us ask where tone is represented and whether it is used \citep{2025-findings-emnlp-719}.

We make five contributions.
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{\noilai{}}, to our knowledge the first LLM study of nói lái and the first generative, decoding and validity benchmark of it: a rule-verified generator over the four generated variants of a six-type tradition, an attested cultural subset, and a native-validation and human-baseline protocol (Section~\ref{sec:benchmark}).
  \item \textbf{A syllable-alignment audit} of how each production tokenizer splits onset, rime and tone, linked to item-level accuracy across \placeholder{21} models (Sections~\ref{sec:setup} and~\ref{sec:results}).
  \item \textbf{Orthographic counterfactuals} that change only the token sequence of a tonal Latin script on deployed models, with a census of which tokenizers normalize, pass through or corrupt combining marks (Section~\ref{sec:counterfactuals}).
  \item \textbf{A mechanistic localization} of tone in small open models, with control-validated probes, structural baselines and activation patching under separate perception and manipulation readouts (Section~\ref{sec:tone}).
  \item \textbf{Reusable tooling}: NFC/NFD re-encoding and diacritic-stripping tools that apply to any diacritic-heavy Latin script; and a Vietnamese syllable parser and spelling-rule engine, released with the generator, which, like the tone-mark placement and partially composed arms, encode Vietnamese phonotactics and spelling and are not script-general.
\end{enumerate}
