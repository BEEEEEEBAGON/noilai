\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

We asked whether subword LLMs can reach the onset, rime and tone inside a Vietnamese syllable, and used a rule-generated wordplay benchmark, meaning-preserving re-encodings and small-model probes to answer it from three directions. \placeholder{Summary of the answers to \hyp{1}--\hyp{6} in three sentences, written from the results.}

Three points hold whatever the numbers turn out to be. First, a benchmark whose gold is computed rather than annotated can be regenerated after any leak and scored without an LLM judge, which makes its uncertainty estimates cheap and its comparisons paired; we think this is the right shape for probing sub-token competence in any rule-governed script. Second, the two re-encodings are interventions available on every deployed model at zero training cost, and the normalization census that must precede them is itself a useful piece of documentation: whether a tokenizer maps NFD onto NFC decides whether a whole class of Vietnamese text reaches the model as intended. Third, the decomposition of an intervention effect into a form-only part and a token-count part is descriptive, and we have resisted calling it mediation; the causal claim is confined to the item-level contrast, where meaning is held fixed by construction.
