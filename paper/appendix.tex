\section{Prompts}
\label{app:prompts}

Every prompt is rendered from a YAML template by the evaluation harness; the three paraphrases (p0, p1, p2) carry the same content in different wording and order, and the English-instruction ablation changes only the instruction language while the item and the answer marker \vi{đáp án:} stay Vietnamese. Demonstrations are built by the rule engine from three fixed base pairs (\vi{trung bình}, \vi{làm chủ}, \vi{vô hình}) whose syllables occur in no item; \task{T2} demonstrations are the same for every item because the prompt withholds the variant. No model receives a system prompt; the three demonstrations are inline in the single user turn. Decoding is greedy with a 64-token cap, and a claim about a model holds only if its direction holds under all three paraphrases, whose range is reported beside the mean. The ablations are zero-shot, name-only (the variant's name without its steps), English-instruction and character-spaced versions of the p0 prompt, each run on the core for the self-hosted models; the explicit onset--rime--tone decomposition of each syllable is a main condition, run on the core for every self-hosted model. The examples below are the p0 templates with three demonstrations, exactly as rendered, for one example item per task; the sha256 of every prompt sent to a model is stored with its output. Extraction normalizes the output to NFC, removes and counts any thinking block (which must be empty in the main runs), takes the capture after the last answer marker, the rest of its line or, when that is empty, the next non-empty line (or after the fallback markers \vi{trả lời:} and \vi{kết quả:}, or a two-syllable last line), strips markdown, quotes and a leading variant label, and records the extraction method; for \task{T3} a leading \vi{không} (`not') decides ``no'' before any yes-word, a hedge between two candidates is unparseable, and a capture with no yes/no token is unparseable. \placeholder{[NATIVE-CHECK] Every Vietnamese string awaits validation by a native speaker before the runs; the six-type overview and the region-neutral wording of DESIGN\_DECISIONS 3 are to be reflected in the templates.}

\section{Rule Tables}

The tables in this appendix are generated from the parser's constants and the spelling lists, so that they cannot drift from the code: Table~\ref{tab:components} lists the canonical symbols, Table~\ref{tab:onsets} the surface spelling of every onset, Table~\ref{tab:spelling} the remaining spelling rules with engine-spelled examples, Table~\ref{tab:placement} the 69 syllables whose tone-mark letter differs between the two placement conventions, and Table~\ref{tab:variants} the six variants with engine-computed examples and the status of each (the four generated \task{T1} cells; \var{5}, the onset swap, and \var{6}, onsets and tones, are the order-reversed readings of \var{4} and \var{1} and enter the taxonomy, the \task{T2} gold and the \task{T3} twins). Both placement conventions share three rules (one vowel letter: on it; a consonant coda: on the last vowel letter; three vowel letters without a coda: on the middle one) and differ only on two-letter open rimes, where the older convention marks the first letter unless the second carries a quality diacritic and the newer one also marks the second letter of \vi{oa}, \vi{oe}, \vi{uy}; the rules reproduce every parsable syllable of the two Hunspell lists. In NFD the tone mark precedes the quality mark on \vi{ộ ậ ặ ệ} and follows the horn on \vi{ớ ứ ở ữ ờ ừ}, so the tone mark is not always the final code point; the bytes of a toned letter also differ by tone (every \vi{hỏi} and \vi{nặng} letter is three bytes in UTF-8), which is why every analysis stratifies by tone class and vowel block.

\section{Build, Filters and Sampling}
\label{app:build}

\paragraph{Base pairs.}
Lexical base pairs are the two-syllable entries of the Viet74K word list (\releaseNLexicalPairsAvailable{} distinct canonical pairs from 49,103 two-syllable entries) whose syllables are both in the attested inventory, minus \vi{qu}- inputs, \vi{p}-onset and marginal-rime syllables, any pair whose input or any variant output contains a blocklisted syllable or pair, any pair equal in either order to an attested input or output, and any pair sharing a syllable with the demonstration pairs. Pseudo-pairs are two attested syllables drawn uniformly and accepted by quota so that, over the base-pair pool, the five strata (zero onset; glide; stop coda; a \vi{c/k}, \vi{g/gh} or \vi{ng/ngh} trigger on an output syllable; tone-pair class \vi{bằng}--\vi{bằng}, \vi{bằng}--\vi{trắc}, \vi{trắc}--\vi{trắc}) match the lexical pairs' joint marginals, with the priority tone class, stop coda, trigger, glide, onset when constraints conflict and a cap on the total attempts of 50 times the number of pseudo pairs, over the whole pool; the achieved shares, every shortfall and the scope of the quota are written into the manifest (in \releaseVersion{} every share is within 0.01 of its target and no quota fell short). Marginal rimes are excluded by rule, never by hand list: a rime with fewer than four syllable types in the spelling list, or whose every type is a loan or onomatopoeia (\releaseNMarginalRimes{} rimes in \releaseVersion{}, named in the manifest); the standard rimes \vi{uơ} and \vi{ưi} are kept regardless of count. Zero-onset bare /i/ syllables are excluded because the lexicon has no form for three of their six tones.

\paragraph{Filters and drop counts.}
Filters run in a fixed order and the manifest records the drop count per cell and per rule: (1) legality of every output syllable (onset plus rime attested, stop codas with \vi{sắc} or \vi{nặng} only); (2) the spell-then-parse round trip; (3) outputs equal to the input or to its plain reversal (over the \releaseNBasePairs{} base pairs of \releaseVersion{}: \releaseDropIdentity{} identities, \releaseDropReversal{} reversals, mirror images across \var{1}/\var{6}, \var{2}/\var{3} and \var{4}/\var{5} as the algebra requires, and \releaseDropIllegal{} illegal outputs); (4) duplicate output strings for one base pair, merged under the set of variant labels that produce them, the item being filed under the lowest-numbered label; (5) the blocklist (\releaseNVulgar{} items flagged, \releaseDropVulgarInput{} input pairs dropped as taboo phrases); (6) attested-set and demonstration overlap (\releaseNReservedPairs{} reserved pairs, \releaseNReservedSyllables{} reserved syllables); (7) the \vi{qu}-, \vi{p}, marginal-rime and loan exclusions. Because filing \var{1}$\equiv$\var{4} outputs under \var{1} removes same-tone pairs from the \var{4} cell while \var{1} keeps them, per-variant accuracies differ in tone-pair composition by construction; the non-degenerate subset is therefore the primary basis for any variant-vs-variant claim, and the manifest carries per-cell degenerate counts.

\paragraph{Covariates.}
Every item records input and output lexicality, whether every output syllable is attested, glide, zero onset, stop coda, the spelling triggers applied, the tone pair, equal tones, onsets or rimes, the degenerate flag, the variant labels, the emitted \vi{i/y} form of each syllable (it changes token counts: in the Gemma~3 vocabulary \vi{sĩ}, \vi{kỹ} and \vi{mỹ} are one piece and \vi{sỹ}, \vi{kĩ}, \vi{mĩ} two), overlap with an attested row added after the freeze, whether the placement convention affects it, the word-list frequency of each syllable, and, for \task{T2}, the number of readings; per-tokenizer covariates (tokens per syllable, split, alignment among split syllables, tone isolation under NFD, byte fallback) are computed by the item audit and joined at scoring time.

\paragraph{Splits, canary, seed.}
The pool is split by base pair (development \releaseDevPercent{}\%, test the rest; a flagged item moves its whole base pair to the test split). The core is 125 items per \task{T1}/\task{T2} cell and 62 yes/no pairs per \task{T3} cell inside the test split; the main sample is a seeded stratified file of \releaseMainPerCell{} items per cell containing the core (public sampling seed \releaseMainSeed{}), the placement-enriched set a separate seeded build from an independent pseudo pool (seed \releaseCtwoSeed{}), and the API paraphrase subset a derived file of 25 items per \task{T1}/\task{T2} cell and 12 pairs per \task{T3} cell; every file is referenced by SHA-256 in the run plan, never drawn at run time. Each test file begins with a JSON header record carrying the BIG-bench canary sentence and the canary GUID, which loaders skip by key; each test item carries the GUID and the flags \texttt{do\_not\_train} and \texttt{evaluation\_only}; the GUID is generated from a secure source, not from the build seed, and the content hash is defined over the items with the canary removed, so that regenerating a split from its seed reproduces the committed hash. The build seed lives in a private manifest only.

\section{Validation Protocol and Human Baseline}
\label{app:validation}

\paragraph{Validation.}
A probability sample of 1,000 items with known stratum weights over the twelve task $\times$ variant cells and the covariate strata is assigned so that every item is judged by two of three validators and a 200-item overlap by all three; to it are added every core \task{T2} gold set, every attested row, the 20-pair \vi{qu}- convention check, a 20-pair ``which variant do you produce spontaneously?'' item on pairs with unequal tones (so that \var{1} and \var{4} are discriminable) and the placement and \vi{i/y} convention checks. For each item the validator answers with \emph{yes}, \emph{no} or \emph{unsure}: (1) \emph{correct}: the output is the correct permutation of the input under the named variant (for \task{T3}, the system's verdict is right); (2) \emph{spelling}: the output is spelled acceptably, either tone-mark placement and the \vi{lí/lý} alternation being acceptable; (3) \emph{lexical}: the input, and separately the output, is a real Vietnamese word or phrase (not dictionary membership); (4) \emph{offensive}: the item has an offensive or vulgar reading; for \task{T2} items, which readings they accept; and a region tag per validator. Agreement is Krippendorff's nominal $\alpha$ with a bootstrap interval, reported with raw agreement, the marginal distribution and Gwet's AC1, and with MASI and Jaccard distances for the set-valued \task{T2} gold; the illustration quoted in Section~\ref{sec:validation} is a simulation with the released agreement code on 200,000 units at 96\% prevalence (1\% independent error per coder: raw agreement 0.980, $\alpha$ 0.788, AC1 0.978; 2\%: 0.961, 0.645, 0.956). An item on which the two assigned validators disagree goes to the third; a remaining disagreement is adjudicated by the authors with a logged reason; a disagreement pattern that points at a rule triggers a review of the rule tables, and a confirmed generator bug triggers a fix and a full regeneration before any model run, which is reported. Items the majority judges \emph{unsure} stay in the benchmark and out of the precision estimate. Generator precision per cell is the weighted share of items whose majority judgment on (1) is \emph{yes}, with a clustered interval. Validators are identified by a letter; the instructions, in Vietnamese and English, and the consent form are released with the data.

\paragraph{Human baseline.}
About twenty adult native speakers each complete one form of 30 items (about 20 minutes): six anchor items seen by everyone (one per generated variant, one \task{T2}, one \task{T3}) and 24 items assigned so that every non-anchor item is seen by exactly two people, 246 items in all, about twenty per cell, drawn from the open-model main sample so that every model is scored on exactly these items; items flagged vulgar and spelling twins are excluded, \task{T3} items are half yes and half no and never both members of a twin pair. The 240 double-judged items are dealt in twelve blocks of twenty to the twenty respondents twice, the second copy with a block-specific cyclic offset, so that no respondent sees an item twice and every one of the 190 rater pairs shares one item (140 pairs) or two (50 pairs): the rater graph is connected, whereas a fixed offset would produce ten disjoint dyads. Respondents see the same p0 prompt text and the same three demonstrations as the models, in randomized item order, with one instruction-check item, and write the answer (\task{T1}, \task{T2}) or tick yes/no (\task{T3}); they are asked not to use a dictionary, a search engine or an AI assistant and to say whether they did; a further ten-item block gives attested, non-vulgar \task{T2} items as the human reference for the cultural task (about 25 minutes in all). Answers are scored by the same code as the models, strict, lenient and tolerant of the \vi{ay/ây} and regional-homophone doublets. Item-level human accuracy is the mean of its two judgments; the comparator is mean-human accuracy, with ``any human correct'' as a ceiling reported separately and an interval from a two-way bootstrap over persons and items; results are reported by output lexicality and by region (the \vi{hỏi}/\vi{ngã} and \vi{-n}/\vi{-ng} items separately for Southern speakers), and the agreement between the two raters of an item is reported as nominal $\alpha$ on the produced answer and on correctness. Forms with fewer than half of the items answered, respondents who report using a tool, and forms that fail the instruction check are excluded and counted. If more volunteers materialize, items receive a third rater before the item set widens.

\section{Scoring, Census and Statistics}
\label{app:methods}

\paragraph{Error taxonomy.}
Every \task{T1} output receives one class in this precedence: \emph{unparseable} (no extractable answer, not two syllables, a hedge, or truncated before the marker); \emph{correct}; \emph{lenient-only} (the gold syllables in the other order); \emph{copy} of the input; plain \emph{reversal}; \emph{wrong variant} (another of the six variants' output, which is recorded); \emph{spelling} (the gold structure under a non-strict parse: \vi{c/k}, \vi{g/gh}, \vi{ng/ngh} or an \vi{i/y} outside the equivalence contexts); \emph{homophone} (a fixed list of regional spellings, \vi{d/gi}, \vi{ch/tr}, \vi{s/x}, \vi{r/d}); \emph{doublet} (a fixed list of \vi{ay/ây} lexical doublets such as \vi{giày/giầy}); \emph{component} (a legal structure differing in a listed onset, rime, tone or order); \emph{illegal}. \task{T2} adds \emph{plausible-non-gold}; \task{T3} and XCOPA add \emph{wrong} for an answer of the other label. Rates of every class are reported per model, task and variant.

\paragraph{Census.}
For every panel tokenizer and every engine path it runs through (native SentencePiece on the model file, the Hugging Face \texttt{tokenizers} library on \texttt{tokenizer.json}, vLLM's and llama.cpp's prompt token ids, or a provider's token counter), on the fixed probe set of 500 multi-syllable words and 500 \noilai{} inputs, the ids of the NFC, NFD and partially composed forms are recorded and the path is classified as passing through, normalizing or corrupting; ``decoding returns the input'' means that the NFC form of the decoded string equals the NFC form of the input. SentencePiece families have native and library ids asserted equal; byte-level BPE families have library and engine ids asserted equal; hosted providers are censused behaviourally. A normalizing tokenizer has its NFD and partially composed arms skipped and a ``0 by construction'' row emitted; a corrupting path has the arm refused on that engine, and any effect is attributed to the string the model actually received. The verdict is copied into every run manifest. The three meaning-preserving arms re-encode the whole prompt (instruction, demonstrations, item and answer marker) under the primary scope; the item-only scope, in which the instruction and demonstrations stay NFC, is a secondary condition run on a 500-item subset of one model, and the strip arms are item-only under both scopes, since stripping the instruction would destroy it. Normalization is engine-dependent (a library normalizer can delete combining marks that the native tokenizer keeps), which is why C1 is also run through two engines on a 500-item subset of Gemma~3 1B and the agreement reported.

\paragraph{Intervals and tests.}
Cluster bootstrap by base pair, 2,000 replicates, fixed seed, percentile intervals (BCa when a cell has at least 50 base pairs), stratified by base-pair type (lexical vs.\ pseudo); every comparison paired by item inside each replicate; $p$-values from the same bootstrap or from a paired $t$ on base-pair-level mean differences (at least 200 base pairs), the primary inference for every paired contrast. McNemar's exact mid-$p$ test needs binary paired outcomes and is undefined on paraphrase-aggregated proportions, so it is a secondary p0 item-level statistic for single-model binary contrasts, labelled as ignoring clustering, with a GEE clustered by base pair as the cluster-aware version. Scoring modes (generation vs.\ log-probability) and panels (open vs.\ API) are never tested against each other. Cells at 0\% or 100\% or with fewer than 20 base pairs get Wilson intervals on the effective sample size. Every cell reports point estimate, interval, number of items, number of base pairs, realized design effect and unparseable rate. Holm families: Table~\ref{tab:arms}, one family per model row, the arms NFD, PC, tone-stripped and diacritic-stripped on \task{T1}, \task{T3} and XCOPA of the main sample plus the placement arm on \task{T1} and \task{T3} of the C2-enriched set, fourteen cells minus those undefined for the model (a normalizing tokenizer's NFD and PC cells are ``0 by construction'' rows, not tests; API models have no XCOPA log-likelihood cell); \hyp{3}, a family of two per pass-through model; \hyp{3b}, the four difference-in-differences contrasts; \hyp{4} and \hyp{6}, per-model tests across the panel; Table~\ref{tab:main}, the ablations, the explicit-input condition, probes and patching are estimation only. Pooled estimates are hierarchical (model in tokenizer family: Gemma SentencePiece, Qwen BPE, Llama~3.1, PhoGPT, Vistral, o200k, Gemini), REML with the Hartung--Knapp adjustment or a family-level bootstrap, reported with $k$ of $N$ families; for \hyp{3}, with fewer than five informative families the pooled interval is labelled unreliable and the per-family intervals decide.

\paragraph{The fragmentation regression.}
Covariates are item $\times$ tokenizer quantities with a within--between decomposition: tokens per syllable minus the model's mean, the model's mean, a split indicator, alignment among split syllables (zero when unsplit), input and output lexicality, the standardized log frequency of each syllable in a reference corpus used as a proxy for training exposure (the union of a news-crawl and a web sample; the word-list count is a secondary column), the emitted \vi{i/y} forms, the whole-syllable-token share of the tokenizer, variant and task. Reference specification, paraphrases aggregated to binomial counts per item and model: $\mathrm{cbind}(k, 3-k) \sim 1 + \mathrm{split} + \mathrm{tps}_w + \mathrm{align}_w + \mathrm{lexical}_{\mathrm{in}} + \mathrm{lexical}_{\mathrm{out}} + \mathrm{logfreq}_{1} + \mathrm{logfreq}_{2} + \mathrm{variant} + \mathrm{task} + (1 + \mathrm{split} + \mathrm{tps}_w + \mathrm{align}_w \mid \mathrm{model}) + (1 \mid \mathrm{base\ pair}/\mathrm{item})$. The primary fit is a logistic regression with model fixed effects and model $\times$ fragmentation interactions, standard errors and intervals from the base-pair cluster bootstrap (two-way sandwich errors over 21 model clusters only as a sensitivity); \hyp{1} is decided by the nested likelihood-ratio test or $\Delta$AIC of the model with tokens per syllable against the model that adds the split indicator and alignment; a model with fewer than 300 misaligned split syllables in the main sample contributes descriptively only, and if fewer than four families clear that floor \hyp{1} is a descriptive result. The random-slope model is the generalization check (variational point estimates are used for screening only), a GEE clustered by base pair the robustness fit; the fit is reported with and without the frequency term.

\paragraph{Intervention estimands.}
For arm $a$ and model $m$, $\tau_m(a) = \mathbb{E}_i[Y_i(a) - Y_i(\mathrm{base})]$ over the sampled items, with a base-pair cluster-bootstrap interval and a paired $t$ on base-pair means. The ``share mediated by token count'' is not estimated: within an item and a tokenizer the arm determines the token sequence deterministically, token count is a coarsening of it, and the cross-world counterfactual corresponds to no manipulable state. In its place, in order: effect modification by the item's token-count change, adjusted for baseline difficulty, the number of tone and quality marks, syllable frequency and length, variant, lexicality, glide and spelling trigger, labelled associational; the zero-dose contrast within a tokenizer (changed ids, unchanged count), where a stratum holds at least 100 items per model; the three-arm ordered contrast baseline $<$ PC $<$ NFD in token count; and the encoding $\times$ character-spacing $2 \times 2$ where spacing saturates the count. \hyp{3b} matches items within base-pair strata on syllable frequency and NFC and NFD token counts, with at least 500 items per cell. \hyp{6} matches each exact attested item to three to five generated items from base pairs outside the attested set, exactly on variant, task, output lexicality, spelling-trigger presence and tone-pair class and nearest on log frequency and syllable length (coarsened exact matching or Mahalanobis with a 0.2-SD caliper, standardized mean differences below 0.1 after matching); the contamination discriminators are the guided-instruction test (source cue plus input against the rule instruction, matched generated items as the null), closed-book recall of well-known nói lái, the conditional log-probability contrast and the name-only prompt. XCOPA is scored by the summed token log-probability of each alternative (primary) and the per-token mean (secondary), because NFD lengthens the alternatives unequally; the alternatives' token-count difference is a covariate.

\paragraph{Power.}
At 1,000 independent items and 70\% accuracy a 95\% interval has half-width 2.84 points; under base-pair clustering with $m$ items per pair and intraclass correlation $\rho$, the design effect is $1 + (m-1)\rho$ (3.4 points at $m = 3$, $\rho = 0.2$; 4.0 at $m = 6$, $\rho = 0.2$; 6.6 at $m = 12$, $\rho = 0.4$). Power for a paired 5-point difference at $n = 1{,}000$ is 0.94 at 20\% discordance, 0.81 at 30\%, 0.68 at 40\% and 0.65 at 44\% (70\% vs.\ 65\% with independent errors). Items per cell for a 10-point paired contrast at 80\% power: 100, 145, 227, 308 and 356 at 15, 20, 30, 40 and 46\% discordance, times the design effect; hence 350 items per cell in the main sample. The 125-item API cells resolve 11--17 points; XCOPA's 500 paired items resolve 2.7, 3.9 and 5.5 points at flip rates of 5, 10 and 20\%; the 200-item pilot has $\pm$6--7 points. The pilot's intraclass correlation by cell and discordance between paraphrases and between models fix $n$ before any test run; if pilot discordance exceeds 35\%, cell-level claims are dropped or $n$ is raised, never afterwards.

\section{Data Statement and Release Identity}
\label{app:datastatement}

Following \citet{Q18-1041}. \emph{Curation rationale}: items are generated by a rule engine from two-syllable entries of a Vietnamese word list and from sampled attested syllables, to test whether models can manipulate the components of a syllable; attested examples are collected from published collections of folk and literary nói lái, each with its source. \emph{Language variety}: standard written Vietnamese (\texttt{vi-VN}), lowercase, NFC, stored in the majority placement convention (\releasePlacementStyle{} style), \vi{i/y} per the stated rule; the attested subset includes Southern forms. \emph{Speaker demographics}: not applicable to generated items; attested items are folk material of unknown authorship or literary quotations with their source cited; \section{Pre-registration}
\label{app:prereg}

The pre-registration is two-stage. The generator, data build, sampling files, scoring, extraction, census, placement baseline and constants are committed first (commit \placeholder{stage-1 hash}); the hypotheses of Sections~\ref{sec:results}--\ref{sec:tone}, the metrics, exclusion rules, stopping rules, analysis plan and probing protocol are fixed before any test-split run (the pilot runs on development items only) and committed on \placeholder{date of the stage-2 commit} (commit \placeholder{stage-2 hash}); deviations are logged with a date in the repository. Table~\ref{tab:hypotheses} restates the hypotheses with their directional predictions and the quantity that decides each. The pilot's go/no-go rule is one criterion: for at least one of three pilot models, strict \task{T1} accuracy on 200 development items exceeds the copy baseline by at least 15 points with the base-pair bootstrap interval of the difference excluding zero; the re-encoding arms continue at full scale if, for at least one pass-through pilot model, the paired baseline--NFD difference on \task{T1} or XCOPA is at least 5 points with McNemar mid-$p < 0.05$, over six pre-specified tests, uncorrected at the pilot stage and all reported (the criterion is written once, in section 10 of the registration document).

\paragraph{Exclusion rules.}
(1) Model outputs are never excluded; unparseable, empty, refused and truncated outputs count as wrong and are reported per model and prompt. (2) An item is removed only when the validators' majority marks the gold incorrect and the cause is a confirmed generator bug; the fix triggers a full regeneration before any model run and every affected run is repeated; there is no post hoc item exclusion. (3) Vulgar-flagged items leave the public development split, every API prompt and every human form, and stay flagged in the gated test file. (4) An item sharing a syllable or a canonical phrase with the demonstration pairs or the attested set is excluded at build, and the runner refuses such a file. (5) A model is dropped only for a documented engineering failure (it fails the 20-item smoke test after two configuration attempts) and is named with the reason; a partial run is reported on the items it completed, flagged; a corrupting tokenizer path has the affected arm refused on that engine. (6) A disagreement above 5 points between a quantized or fp16 run and its bf16 re-run on about 200 items flags the run, which is not excluded. (7) Human-baseline forms with fewer than 15 of 30 items answered, respondents who report using a dictionary, a search engine or an AI assistant, and failed instruction checks are excluded and counted. (8) Probe layers are never excluded; patching pairs are filtered only by the pre-specified filters, with retention reported at each.

\paragraph{Stopping rules.}
The generator is frozen at the stage-1 commit; the data freeze records the item hashes (Table~\ref{tab:hashes}). Model runs stop at a results freeze; runs finished later enter the appendix only, and no run is repeated because of its result. Sample sizes are fixed in advance; the pilot may raise them before any test-set run, never afterwards. A hosted model that cannot complete the core by the results freeze is reported on the items it completed, flagged. The hypotheses are not modified after the stage-2 commit; a hypothesis suggested by the data is reported as exploratory. The probing and patching study is reported regardless of outcome, in the main text if pair retention allows and in the appendix with the retention table if not. The panel is frozen only after every family passes its smoke test; the minimum viable panel is eight open models over four tokenizer families with a pass-through family other than Gemma, below which the between-tokenizer claims are dropped; the cut order under a compute overrun is Gemma~3 12B, Qwen3.5-0.8B and Gemma~4 12B first, then the second model of any family, then paraphrases, and every cut model is named.

\section{Compute, Environment and Panel Details}
