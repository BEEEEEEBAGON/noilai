\section{Benchmark Construction Details}
\label{app:construction}

\paragraph{Parser, speller and legality.}
The parser maps an orthographic syllable to a structure (onset, glide, nucleus, coda, tone) in canonical symbols, and the speller maps a structure back to standard orthography, applying every spelling rule of Appendix~\ref{app:rules}. Parsing is a spelling round-trip: a string is a well-formed syllable only if the standard spelling of its structure reproduces it, so \vi{céo} (for \vi{kéo}) is rejected in strict mode and recognized, in lenient mode, as a misspelling of a known structure. Two attested alternations are accepted on input and canonicalized: the \vi{i/y} variation after a consonant (\vi{lý} for \vi{lí}) and either tone-mark placement; the emitted \vi{i/y} form follows usage (\placeholder{convention confirmed with the validators}). Legality is decided against an attested inventory, not generated from phonotactic rules: the Hunspell vi\_VN list in both placement styles \citep{hunspell-vi}, extended with word-list syllables that parse strictly and reuse an already attested rime (\placeholder{261} additions; the \placeholder{16} distinct list entries the parser does not parse, 32 lines over the two placement files, and the \placeholder{3} level-toned stop-coda entries the inventory rejects are listed and explained in the data statement). A structure is legal when its onset plus rime occurs in the inventory with some tone and its tone respects the stop-coda constraint; the generator never emits a syllable that fails this test, and every gold answer is re-parsed before it is written. One analytic decision deserves mention: we treat the \vi{u} of \vi{qu} as the glide, as phonology does, whereas school grammar treats \vi{qu} as an onset, and the two analyses give different swaps for a \vi{qu}- syllable. Because no attested example settles it, \vi{qu}- syllables are excluded from generated inputs and kept as a separate stratum pending a 20-pair native check.

\paragraph{Decoding and validity tasks.}
\textbf{\task{T2} (decoding)} gives a nói lái form and asks what meaningful phrase it hides. Since every variant undoes itself, naming the variant would collapse \task{T2} into \task{T1}; the prompt therefore withholds it, and the gold is the set of every lexical reading of the input under any of the six variants in either syllable order. The base pair is always one such reading (asserted at build time); inputs with several readings are kept, and the number of readings is a covariate. Lexicality for the gold is dictionary membership plus native validation of every core item's gold set; the validated set is the primary gold for the core, and a legal answer that is a valid variant reading outside the gold set is binned as plausible-non-gold and its rate reported. The variant the model implicitly applied is scored over the three unordered classes $\{\var{1},\var{6}\}$, $\{\var{2},\var{3}\}$, $\{\var{4},\var{5}\}$, since the finer label is not identifiable from the string.

\textbf{\task{T3} (validity)} pairs each correct \task{T1} output with an invalid twin that shares the input and differs in one component type: the output of another of the six variants when its string is distinct, a changed onset, a changed rime, a changed tone, or a misspelling that violates a \vi{c/k}, \vi{g/gh} or \vi{ng/ngh} rule while keeping the structure. The model judges whether the candidate is the correct output of the named variant. Yes and no items come in pairs with the same input, so the set is balanced by construction and open models can also be scored by forced choice over the pair. Because spelling twins test orthographic convention rather than sub-syllabic access, the headline \task{T3} number excludes them and reports them as their own column; generated answers are also reported as balanced accuracy, so that a bias toward one answer cannot inflate the headline (\placeholder{observed yes-rate per model}). Placement variants are never twins, because both placements are valid.

\paragraph{Sampling, filters and strata.}
Lexical base pairs are the two-syllable entries of a 74k-word list whose syllables are both attested (\placeholder{47,535} candidates; word-list typos and loanwords are dropped by the attestation test); pseudo-pairs are quota-sampled from attested syllables with a fixed seed so that their joint distribution over zero onset, glide, stop coda, spelling trigger and tone-pair class matches that of the lexical base pairs, so that pseudo and lexical items differ only in lexicality; the achieved shares, and any quota that could not be filled, are recorded in the build manifest. Filters run in a fixed order and their drop counts are recorded per cell: legality of every output syllable; the spelling round trip; outputs equal to the input or to its plain reversal (equal tones make \var{3} the identity and \var{2} a plain reversal; equal rimes make \var{1} the identity); duplicate output strings for one base pair, merged with the set of variant labels that produce them; a blocklist screen for vulgar readings, applied per syllable and per syllable pair in either order to outputs, \task{T2} readings and \task{T3} candidates, and to an input only when the input pair itself is a taboo phrase, since a per-syllable input screen would remove ordinary vocabulary and skew the strata; overlap with the attested set or the demonstration pairs; and the \vi{qu}-, \vi{p}-onset, marginal-rime and loanword exclusions. Each item carries covariates that enter every regression: lexicality of input and output, syllable frequencies in a reference corpus (the word-list count as a secondary column), presence of a glide, a zero onset or a stop coda, the spelling triggers applied (e.g.\ \vi{c}$>$\vi{k}), the tone pair and whether the tones, onsets or rimes coincide, and whether a syllable is affected by the placement convention. \task{T1} cells are balanced between lexical and pseudo inputs; \task{T2} is built from lexical pairs only. The pool is split \emph{by base pair}, so that every item derived from one underlying pair falls in one split, into a public development set (20\%) and a gated test set. Open models are run on a stratified \emph{main sample} of 4,200 test items (350 per task $\times$ variant cell, the size that resolves a 10-point cell contrast at 80\% power under the design effect the pilot measures; \placeholder{realized design effect from the pilot}) that contains the balanced \emph{core} of 125 items per cell (\task{T3}: 62 yes/no pairs), the only \noilai{} data an API-served model sees; a separately built placement-enriched set of pseudo-pair items, disjoint from the released splits by base pair and excluded from the main sample, every one of which changes under C2, serves the C2 arm. The canary, the withheld build seed and the sealed split are described in Section~\ref{sec:benchmark}. A sealed split with a different seed and canary is regenerated at release, never sent to any API and never released; only its hash and counts are published. Contamination is a weak threat to a generator that can mint a fresh test set, but it is a real one for the attested subset, which we test with a guided-instruction probe \citep{2308.08493}.

The development split ships under CC~BY~4.0; the test split is gated and encrypted, with its canary, under CC~BY-NC-ND~4.0 (\placeholder{or GPLv2 with attribution if the word-list authors' permission has not arrived by the data freeze; DESIGN\_DECISIONS 4.1}). \section{Validation Protocol and Human Baseline}
\label{app:validation}

\paragraph{Validation.}
A probability sample of 1,000 items with known stratum weights over the twelve task $\times$ variant cells and the covariate strata is assigned so that every item is judged by two of three validators and a 200-item overlap by all three; to it are added every core \task{T2} gold set, every attested row, the 20-pair \vi{qu}- convention check, a 20-pair ``which variant do you produce spontaneously?'' item and the placement and \vi{i/y} convention checks. For each item the validator answers with \emph{yes}, \emph{no} or \emph{unsure}: (1) \emph{correct}: the output is the correct nói lái of the input under the named variant (for \task{T3}, the system's verdict is right); (2) \emph{spelling}: the output is spelled acceptably, either tone-mark placement and the \vi{lí/lý} alternation being acceptable; (3) \emph{lexical}: the input, and separately the output, is a real Vietnamese word or phrase (not dictionary membership); (4) \emph{offensive}: the item has an offensive or vulgar reading; for \task{T2} items, which readings they accept; and a region tag per validator. Agreement is Krippendorff's nominal $\alpha$ with a bootstrap interval, reported with raw agreement, the marginal distribution and Gwet's AC1, and with MASI and Jaccard distances for the set-valued \task{T2} gold. An item on which the two assigned validators disagree goes to the third; a remaining disagreement is adjudicated by the authors with a logged reason; a disagreement pattern that points at a rule triggers a review of the rule tables, and a confirmed generator bug triggers a fix and a full regeneration before any model run, which is reported. Generator precision per cell is the weighted share of items whose majority judgment on (1) is \emph{yes}, with a clustered interval. The instructions given to validators, in Vietnamese and English, and the consent form are released with the data (\anonurl).

\paragraph{Human baseline.}
About twenty adult native speakers each complete one form of 30 items (about 20 minutes): six anchor items seen by everyone (one per generated variant, one \task{T2}, one \task{T3}) and 24 items assigned so that every non-anchor item is seen by exactly two people, 246 items in all, about twenty per cell, drawn from the open-model main sample so that every model is scored on exactly these items; items flagged vulgar are excluded. Respondents see the same p0 prompt text and the same three demonstrations as the models, in randomized item order, with one instruction-check item, and write the answer (\task{T1}, \task{T2}) or tick yes/no (\task{T3}); they are asked not to use a dictionary, a search engine or an AI assistant and to say whether they did. Answers are scored by the same code as the models. Item-level human accuracy is the mean of its two judgments; the comparator is mean-human accuracy, with ``any human correct'' as a ceiling reported separately and an interval from a two-way bootstrap over persons and items; results are reported by output lexicality and by region, and the agreement between the two raters of an item is reported as nominal $\alpha$ on the produced answer and on correctness. Forms with fewer than half of the items answered are excluded and counted. If more volunteers materialize, items receive a third rater before the item set widens.

\section{Data Statement}
\label{app:datastatement}

Following \citet{Q18-1041}. \emph{Curation rationale}: items are generated by a rule engine from two-syllable entries of a Vietnamese word list and from sampled attested syllables, to test whether models can manipulate the components of a syllable; attested examples are collected from published collections of folk and literary nói lái, each with its source. \emph{Language variety}: standard written Vietnamese (\texttt{vi-VN}), lowercase, NFC, stored in the majority placement convention, \vi{i/y} per the stated rule; the attested subset includes Southern forms. \emph{Speaker demographics}: not applicable to generated items; attested items are folk material of unknown authorship or literary quotations with their source cited; the attested curation reflects the author's region. \emph{Annotator demographics}: three adult native speakers and about twenty human-baseline respondents, described in aggregate by age band, region grown up in, years in Vietnam and whether raised abroad (\placeholder{reported at submission}). \emph{Speech situation}: written wordplay, out of context. \emph{Text characteristics}: two-syllable phrases (three in a few attested cases), no punctuation, with per-syllable structure, lexicality and frequency covariates. \emph{Provenance}: the spelling list and word list (GPLv2), XCOPA (CC~BY~4.0), per-row citations for attested items, the generator commit and content hash (the build seed is withheld), resource hashes; the SHA-256 of the canary GUID \placeholder{printed here at release}, the GUID itself living in the gated README, the dataset card and the file header; split sizes and vulgar-flag counts; the rule-versus-folk mismatches and out-of-design rows of the attested set; the list entries the parser rejects, listed and explained; known limitations (\vi{qu}, regional labels, the \vi{i/y} convention); and the full parse table of the syllable inventory as supplementary material. The long-form statement is released with the data (\anonurl).

\section{Analysis Details}
\label{app:analysis}

\paragraph{Panel rationale.}
The panel of \placeholder{17} self-hosted and \placeholder{4} API-served models is chosen to maximize the number of distinct tokenizers while pairing general multilingual models with Southeast Asian and Vietnamese-specialized models of matched size. the table below lists the groups: an interpretability ladder of Gemma~3 instruction-tuned models (1B, 4B, 12B), whose every layer is covered by Gemma Scope~2 \citep{gemma-team-2025-gemma3,lieberum-etal-2024-gemma-scope,deepmind-2025-gemma-scope-2}; scaling series within two open families (Qwen~3.5 at four sizes, Gemma~4 at three); Southeast Asian fine-tunes (SEA-LION v4.5 E2B, Sailor2-8B); Vietnamese-first models (PhoGPT-4B-Chat with its 20,480-token vocabulary \citep{2311.02945}, Vistral-7B-Chat); a legacy baseline without official Vietnamese support (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct); two 27B open references served on a TPU; and four hosted models (two Gemini Flash variants, gpt-oss-120b and gpt-oss-20b) that return generations but no log-probabilities. A fine-tune whose tokenizer file is byte-identical to its base's (SEA-LION E2B vs.\ Gemma~4 E2B) holds tokenization fixed while varying training data; with $n = 1$ such a pair suggests, never isolates. The panel is frozen only after every family passes its smoke test, with every checkpoint identifier and revision resolved; exact identifiers, serving engine, precision and quantization are recorded per run in a manifest and listed in Appendix~\ref{app:compute}. Gemma~3 is never run in fp16, whose activations overflow; no model receives a system prompt; chat templates are rendered in the runner so that the hashed string is exactly what every backend scores; thinking modes are detected from the template and off in the main runs; and about 200 items per quantized model are re-run in bf16 to measure drift.

\paragraph{Prompting and ablations.}
Prompts are written in Vietnamese and define the syllable structure, the variant step by step and the spelling rules, followed by three fixed demonstrations built by the engine from three base pairs that occur in no item (Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}). Three paraphrases of every template carry the same content in different wording and order, since formatting alone has moved accuracies by tens of points \citep{2310.11324}; results are reported as the mean over paraphrases with the range. Ablations use zero-shot, name-only (the variant's name without its steps, separating cultural recall from rule-following) and English-instruction versions, and a character-spaced input format. A further input format, the explicit onset--rime--tone decomposition of each syllable, is a main condition rather than an ablation: it is the only one that separates rule knowledge from sub-token access. Decoding is greedy with a 64-token cap. A regex extracts the last line beginning \vi{đáp án:} (`answer:'), after any thinking block is removed and counted; an output without an extractable answer counts as wrong, never as missing.

\paragraph{Re-encoding arms and scope.}
The baseline is NFC text in the majority (older) placement convention. C1 rewrites the text from NFC to NFD; C1$'$ rewrites it into the partially composed Windows-1258 form (\texttt{pc}), which adds fewer tokens than NFD, so that the three arms are ordered in token count; C2 rewrites it into the newer placement convention, which changes exactly the 69 syllable types of Appendix~\ref{app:rules} and nothing else and is estimated on affected items; the contrast arms C3a and C3b strip the tone marks, or every diacritic, from the item text, destroying information. The three meaning-preserving arms are applied to the entire prompt (instruction, demonstrations and their answers, item and answer marker), so that the intervention is uniform, the model sees the demonstration answers in the encoding it must produce, and the item is not the one oddly encoded span in an otherwise normal message, which would itself be a cue; applying them to the item text only is the secondary condition, run on a 500-item subset of one model as an agreement check between the two scopes. The strip arms are item-only under both scopes, because a stripped instruction would confound reading the instruction with reading the item and the answer marker must survive for extraction. A run whose two arms produce byte-identical prompts is skipped and recorded as unchanged. Before any GPU time, a \emph{census} classifies every panel tokenizer on every engine path it runs through as \emph{passing through} (NFC and NFD receive different ids and decode back to the input), \emph{normalizing} (identical ids; the C1 effect is then zero by construction and is asserted, not tested) or \emph{corrupting} (decoding does not return the input; the arm is refused on that engine, since it would turn C1 into C3); native and library tokenizations of the same model are asserted equal. The census column of Table~\ref{tab:arms} is a first-class result.

\paragraph{Scoring and error taxonomy.}
\task{T1} compares parsed structures after a canonicalization that normalizes Unicode form, tone-mark placement, case and the \vi{lí/lý} alternation but deliberately does not repair misspellings and does not equate regional homophones. Every output receives one error class in a fixed precedence: unparseable, correct, lenient-only (right syllables, wrong order), copy of the input, plain reversal, the output of another of the six variants, a spelling violation on the right structure, a regional homophone spelling (\vi{d/gi}, \vi{ch/tr}, \vi{s/x}), an \vi{ay/ây} doublet, a component error naming the wrong onset, rime or tone, or an illegal syllable; per-component accuracy comes from the same parse at no annotation cost. \task{T2} scores membership in the gold set and the unordered variant class. \task{T3} scores the generated yes/no answer for every model, as accuracy and balanced accuracy; for open models we add two log-probability quantities, reported in separate columns and never compared with the generated answer: a yes/no forced choice from the answer-token probabilities after the \task{T3} prompt (\texttt{t3\_yesno\_lp}), and a BLiMP-style paired score comparing the string log-probabilities of the two candidates under the shared \task{T1} prompt (\texttt{t3\_pair\_lp}), which is a likelihood contrast rather than a judgment \citep{2023-acl-long-230,2023-emnlp-main-306}. Because twins differ in spelling and hence in token count and frequency (\vi{mài kéo} vs.\ \vi{mài céo}), the paired score favours the shorter or more frequent string; we report it raw and length-normalized per token, with a breakdown by twin type. No metric uses an LLM judge, whose agreement across languages is poor \citep{2025-findings-emnlp-587}. Baselines are copy, plain reversal, a wrong-variant rule (the informative floor beside copy), a random legal output, the majority answer for \task{T3} and the mean human.

\paragraph{Statistics in full.}
The base pair is the sampled unit and the cluster: every item derived from it, every paraphrase and every model's answers to it form one cluster, and paraphrases are aggregated to the item level. Every interval is a bootstrap over base pairs (2,000 replicates, stratified by pair type, BCa where a cell has at least 50 pairs) \citep{2411.00640}, every comparison is paired by item inside each replicate, and the primary inference for every paired contrast is that bootstrap or a paired $t$ test on base-pair-level mean differences; McNemar's exact mid-$p$ test \citep{mcnemar-1947-note,P18-1128}, which needs binary paired outcomes, is reported only as a secondary item-level statistic on single-model binary contrasts, labelled as ignoring clustering. Holm's step-down correction \citep{holm-1979-simple,Q17-1033} is applied within declared families: for each model, the re-encoding cells of Table~\ref{tab:arms}, the arms NFD, PC, tone-stripped and diacritic-stripped on \task{T1}, \task{T3} and XCOPA of the main sample plus the placement arm on \task{T1} and \task{T3} of the C2-enriched set, fourteen cells minus those undefined for the model, with the realized family size in the table footer; for \hyp{4} and \hyp{6}, the per-model tests across the panel; Table~\ref{tab:main} is estimation only. A claim about models in general rests on a hierarchical pooled estimate with models nested in tokenizer families (REML with the Hartung--Knapp adjustment, or a family-level bootstrap), reported with the number of families it rests on and phrased ``across the panel'', never on a count of significant cells. For what predicts failure (\hyp{1}), item correctness is regressed on tokenization covariates with a within--between decomposition: whether the syllable is split, tokens per syllable centred within model, and alignment among split syllables (alignment is undefined for a single token and collinear with count otherwise), with lexicality, corpus log frequency of each syllable, variant and task; the primary fit is a logistic regression with model fixed effects and model $\times$ fragmentation interactions whose standard errors and intervals come from the base-pair cluster bootstrap (sandwich estimators over 21 model clusters are at most a reported sensitivity), the generalization check a random-slope model, and a GEE clustered by base pair the robustness fit; \hyp{1} is decided by a nested likelihood-ratio test. For the re-encoding arms the headline is the within-item average treatment effect per model; the descriptive decompositions are in Section~\ref{sec:counterfactuals}. At 1,000 independent items and 70\% accuracy the half-width of a 95\% interval is 2.8 points, and under the design effect of base-pair clustering 3.4--5 points; a paired 5-point difference is detected with 94\% power only when few items are discordant, and with about 65\% when errors are independent; 350 items per cell resolve a 10-point contrast at 80\% power, and the 125-item API cells resolve 11--17 points, so cell-level claims rest on the open models. The pilot (200 development items on three models) fixes the intraclass correlation and the discordance rates behind these sizes before any test item is run (\placeholder{pilot ICC and discordance rates}).

\paragraph{Count-mechanism analyses.}
Within one tokenizer, re-encoding an item changes its token sequence deterministically, so token count is a coarsening of the intervention rather than a mediator, and the ``share of the effect mediated by token count'' the founding plan asked for is not an identified quantity; we do not estimate it. Instead, four pre-registered analyses ask whether the count mechanism can account for the effect. First, effect modification: $\tau_m$ as a function of the item's token-count change, adjusted for baseline difficulty, the number of tone and quality marks, syllable frequency and length, variant, lexicality, glide and spelling trigger, labelled associational, since the count change is nearly the number of diacritics. Second, the zero-dose contrast within a tokenizer, items whose count does not change but whose ids do against items whose count grows, reported where the stratum holds at least 100 items per model (realistic for C2 and for vocabularies with NFD pieces, nearly empty for byte-level C1). Third, the three-arm ordered contrast baseline $<$ PC $<$ NFD in token count: if accuracy tracks the extra tokens, PC sits between the two; if NFD moves accuracy beyond what its extra tokens predict, an explicit tone symbol is operating. Fourth, the encoding $\times$ character-spacing $2 \times 2$ from the input-format condition, where spacing saturates the token count for both encodings.

\paragraph{Probes in full.}
Following \citet{2025-findings-emnlp-719}, we train layer-wise linear probes on the residual stream to decode a syllable's tone (and, secondarily, onset, coda, nucleus and rime) at four positions: the onset fragment of a split syllable, the syllable's last token, the bare tone-mark token that NFD sometimes produces, and the first token after the syllable, the one position where NFC and NFD are compared on equal terms. Stimuli are legal syllables balanced by tone, including legal non-word syllables so that tone is not confounded with lexical identity, in several carrier sentences with a tone-neutral word after the slot; train and test syllables are disjoint, with a second split held out by rime. Tone counts as \emph{linearly decodable} at a layer and position only when two conditions hold: \emph{selectivity}, accuracy at least $\delta_{\mathrm{sel}}$ above a control probe trained on random labels per syllable type \citep{D19-1275}, with $\delta_{\mathrm{sel}} = 0.15$ provisional and frozen from the pilot; and \emph{excess} over a structural baseline, the same probe fitted on one-hot token ids plus the coda class, because stop codas admit only two tones and, under NFC, token count itself predicts tone. Probes show that information is present, not that it is used \citep{2022-cl-1-7}; we report a layer-0 baseline curve, and, on syllables that are single tokens, the layer at which tone accuracy first rises to half its maximum gain over the embedding, the tone counterpart of the reconstruction the character-level work reports.

\paragraph{Patching in full.}
Activation patching locates where transplanted states change the output \citep{meng-etal-2022-locating,heimersheim-nanda-2024-use}. A pair is two prompts identical token for token except one syllable whose tone differs, both syllables attested and of equal segmental make-up; the varying syllable is the \emph{second} input syllable, because under \var{3} the first output syllable takes its tone and the two gold answers then diverge at their first token (clean \vi{công tử} $\rightarrow$ \vi{cổng tư}, corrupt \vi{công tự} $\rightarrow$ \vi{cộng tư}: the varying syllables \vi{tử} and \vi{tự} and the readout pieces \vi{cổng} and \vi{cộng} are single Gemma~3 pieces, so both prompts have the same length and the pair passes the filters below; the outputs are pseudo-phrases, \placeholder{[NATIVE-CHECK]}). Pairs are kept only if their tokenizations align, the readout pieces differ in the tone mark alone, and the model's clean answer is correct with a logit gap of at least one nat; a model that loses every pair at the last filter has no manipulation readout, which is itself a result. We cache the clean residual stream, run the corrupt input with layer $\ell$ overwritten at a position group, and read the logit difference between the two answers at the first diverging token, normalized by the clean--corrupt gap. Two readouts separate perception from manipulation: a \emph{perception} readout asks which tone the syllable carries and reads the name of the tone, the logit difference taken on the first Gemma~3 piece of each name (six distinct pieces; \vi{ngã} is read at its first piece); the \emph{manipulation} readout is the \var{3} prompt itself. Position groups are the varying syllable and each of its tokens separately, the fixed syllable, the instruction, the answer marker and the final position; patching everything from the varying syllable onward must recover fully. The $2 \times 2$ of readout by source position distinguishes intact perception, intact manipulation, a copy-type failure (the answer tracks the first input syllable), and an ignore-type failure, in which the perception readout recovers where the manipulation readout does not.
