\section{Where Tone Lives}
\label{sec:tone}

A \var{3} failure may come from not representing a split syllable's tone, not moving it to the answer, or not using it; in Gemma~3 1B and 4B we tell these apart.

\paragraph{Probes and patching.}
Layer-wise linear probes on the residual stream decode a syllable's tone at its last token and at the first token after it, the one position where NFC and NFD are compared on equal terms (Appendix~\ref{app:analysis}). Tone counts as \emph{linearly decodable} only with \emph{selectivity}, accuracy at least $\delta_{\mathrm{sel}} = 0.15$ (provisional) above a random-label control \citep{D19-1275}, and \emph{excess} over a structural baseline fitted on token ids plus the coda class, because stop codas admit two tones and token count itself predicts tone. Patching \citep{meng-etal-2022-locating,heimersheim-nanda-2024-use} uses prompt pairs identical token for token except the tone of the second input syllable, whose \var{3} answers then diverge at the first token (clean \vi{công tử} $\rightarrow$ \vi{cổng tư}, corrupt \vi{công tự} $\rightarrow$ \vi{cộng tư}; all four syllables are single Gemma~3 pieces; \placeholder{[NATIVE-CHECK]}). A \emph{perception} readout asks which tone the syllable carries and reads the tone's name; the \emph{manipulation} readout is the \var{3} prompt itself.

\paragraph{Pre-registered hypothesis.}
\hyp{5} (\emph{perception without manipulation}): in a Gemma~3 model whose \var{3} accuracy is below 25\%, tone meets both decodability conditions at the token after the syllable in the first half of the network, and patching recovers the perception readout where the manipulation readout does not. The manipulation readout is scored on pairs whose clean answer is correct, with retention reported first; if fewer than 50 pairs survive for a model, the perception readout alone is reported as a localization study. No claim of earlier decodability under NFD is made where the structural baseline is near 100\%.
