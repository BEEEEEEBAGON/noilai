\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{Pre-registered hypotheses.}
The predictions below are fixed in a dated commit before any test-split run (Appendix~\ref{app:prereg}; the pilot runs on development items only). \hyp{1} (\emph{alignment beyond count}): in the regression of Section~\ref{sec:setup}, the within-model coefficient of boundary alignment is positive, that of tokens per syllable is negative, and adding the split indicator and alignment to a model that already has tokens per syllable improves its fit, decided by a nested likelihood-ratio test (or $\Delta$AIC) with model fixed effects and base-pair cluster-bootstrap intervals for the coefficients; no ``which matters more'' contrast between the two coefficients is tested, because alignment is structurally zero for an unsplit syllable. A model with fewer than 300 misaligned split syllables in the main sample contributes to \hyp{1} descriptively only, and if fewer than four tokenizer families clear that floor \hyp{1} is demoted to a descriptive result. \hyp{2} (\emph{a whole-syllable vocabulary}) is a descriptive case study, not a tested hypothesis: PhoGPT-4B-Chat, whose 20,480-piece vocabulary stores most syllables as single tokens, is the panel's extreme on the share of whole-syllable tokens, which enters the regression as a per-tokenizer covariate; its per-syllable negative log-likelihood on a held-out Vietnamese text sample and its \task{T1} accuracy are reported with intervals beside Qwen3.5-4B and Gemma~3 4B, and no inequality is pre-registered. \hyp{6} (\emph{memorization}): exact attested items outscore generated items matched on variant, task, output lexicality, spelling trigger and tone class, pooled over models; and the guided-instruction contamination rate is positive on attested items and near zero on the matched generated ones; \hyp{6} is tested only if at least 100 native-verified exact two-syllable attested rows with per-row citations from at least three collections exist at the data freeze, and is otherwise reported descriptively without the matched analysis. The memorization inference rests on the guided-instruction test \citep{2308.08493}, closed-book recall of well-known nói lái, the conditional log-probability contrast and the name-only prompt, not on the accuracy gap alone, which conflates salience with memorization.
