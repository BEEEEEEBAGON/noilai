### PARTICIPANT INFORMATION AND CONSENT FORM (adults, 18 or older)

**Study:** NóiLái — can large language models reach the onset, rime and tone inside Vietnamese syllables?
**Researcher and contact:** `[NAME]`, `[EMAIL]` — *fill in before sending.*

**1. Purpose.** We are building a dataset of *nói lái* wordplay (for example *vô hình → vinh hồ*, which swaps the rimes and keeps the onsets and tones) generated by a program from the spelling rules of Vietnamese, to test whether large language models can perform this wordplay. We need native speakers to (a) check whether the program's answers are correct, or (b) complete a short nói lái form themselves so that we have a human level to compare the models with.

**2. What you will do.**
- *As a validator:* you receive a spreadsheet (opened in Google Sheets) in five parts: A, a 16-row calibration round; B, items generated by the program; C, nói lái collected from books and the web; D, supplementary questions (the nói lái you produce yourself, *qu-* spellings, spelling conventions); E (optional), checking the possible original phrases of some nói lái forms. For each row of Parts A and B you answer with **Yes / No / Unsure**: whether the program's answer is correct; whether it is spelled correctly; whether the phrases are real Vietnamese words or phrases; whether there is a vulgar or offensive reading; and you pick from a list whether your answer depends on how your region pronounces something. You may add a comment. Some rows are **deliberately wrong**, to check attention, and you are told this in advance. Validators do **not** take the human-baseline form below.
- *As a human-baseline respondent:* you answer 30 nói lái questions (produce a nói lái, decode one, or judge one as right or wrong) in one Google Form; each question shows the same instruction and three examples that the language models receive. The form also has one instruction-check item, 10 collected nói lái to decode, and two closing questions: whether you used a dictionary, a search engine or an AI assistant, and whether you were a validator in this study. Please use none of those tools; a form that answers "Yes" to either closing question is not scored. People who were validators do not take this form.

**3. Time.** Validators: about 4–7 hours plus time to read the instructions, in as many sessions as you like: Parts A–D about 4–5.5 hours and Part E (optional) about 1–1.5 hours; with three validators each person is near the lower figure. The calibration round (Part A) is planned for 13–15 October 2026 and Parts B–E for 19 October – 1 November 2026; with three validators, a small number of rows that need a third opinion may follow on 2–5 November 2026. Human-baseline respondents: about 30–45 minutes, once (planned 2–8 November 2026).

**4. Voluntary, unpaid, and your right to withdraw.** Participation is entirely voluntary and **unpaid**. You may stop at any time, skip any item, and need not give a reason. If you want your submitted answers withdrawn, tell us your name, validator letter or form number; the author's private list (item 5c) links them to you. Withdrawal is possible until the results freeze (planned 22 November 2026); after that, published aggregate numbers cannot be changed, but your raw data will still be deleted on request.

**5. What we store.** Three kinds of data: (a) your answers or judgments; (b) a few **coarse** demographic fields used to describe the participant group in the paper: age band (18–29 / 30–49 / 50+), the region you grew up in (North / Centre / South / outside Vietnam), years lived in Vietnam (< 5 / 5–15 / > 15), and whether you were raised abroad; (c) separately from the answers, the author's private list: who was invited, how to contact them, the validator letter or form number (01–20) given, the date consent came back, and the returned consent forms. We do **not** store your name, e-mail address, date of birth, address or anything else that could identify you together with your answers. Validators are identified by a letter (A, B, C) only and baseline respondents by a form number only; the private list (c) is the only key linking letters and form numbers to people. It never goes into the project's code repository or to any AI service, and it is deleted after publication. Raw answers are kept on the author's computer until `[RETENTION — author decides]` and then deleted. If you are a validator, your workbook is a Google Sheet shared with your Google account; while it exists, Google's edit history links your account to your answers. After your last return (no later than 5 November 2026) the author downloads it as an .xlsx file, deletes the Google Sheet and empties the Drive trash; a workbook returned by e-mail is saved the same way and the e-mail deleted.

**6. Risks and benefits.** Some Vietnamese nói lái have vulgar readings. Known vulgar examples are **excluded** from the human-baseline form; if one still bothers you, skip it. The validators' workbook contains some vulgar rows: one calibration row (Part A) deliberately has a common swear word as its output, to check the `offensive` column; a few generated rows (Part B) may be vulgar by accident; and nine of the 119 collected rows in Part C are marked as obscene in our data (sexual words, some of them explicit), taken from bawdy riddles and couplets, jokes retold in newspaper articles, poetry such as Hồ Xuân Hương's, and a dictionary entry. Validators are asked to flag such items and may skip any row. There is no direct benefit; if you wish, you will be named in the paper's *Acknowledgements* (the default is anonymity). With only two or three validators, results reported by region are in effect one person's answers; if you are named in the Acknowledgements, people who know where you grew up may be able to tell which regional results are yours. For that reason the paper and the data release never show a validator's letter next to their region. Apart from these, the only cost to you is your time.

**7. How the data are used.** Judgments are used to estimate the generator's precision and the agreement between validators; human-baseline answers are scored by the same program that scores the models, and everything is published in aggregate form in the paper and in the accompanying open data release. **No personal information is ever sent to a third-party AI service.** Your raw answer sheets are never sent to any AI service either, and they are not put into the project's code repository. In the data release, a verified attested nói lái lists only the letters (A, B, C) of the validators who verified it, never names.

**8. Ethics review.** This study has **not been reviewed by a research ethics board**, because the authors have no access to one. The paper states this. We judge the risk to participants to be minimal: the task is a language judgment on wordplay items, it is anonymous, and you can stop at any time.

## Parental-permission variant — NOT USED

This study recruits **adults only**. The founding plan (Section 2.7) notes that minors could take part only with written parental permission plus their own assent; no such variant is part of this study, no participant under 18 is recruited, and any form returned with the "18 or older" box unticked is discarded unread. If a later study includes minors, a separate permission-plus-assent form must be written and reviewed first.
