## 1. Choosing the paper: ranked ideas and the novelty case

The recommended submission is **Bundle A: one long paper on how LLMs handle Vietnamese sub-syllabic structure under subword tokenization**. It combines a nói lái wordplay benchmark (Idea 2), causal re-encoding experiments on Vietnamese spelling (Idea 7) and a small tone-probing section. The alternative, **Bundle B**, pairs a benchmark of Vietnamese kinship-based address terms (Idea 1) with a mechanism section built on Gemma Scope 2, Google DeepMind's released sparse autoencoders and transcoders for Gemma 3 (Idea 5). B has the stronger acceptance precedent. A is primary because its core idea is the only one of ten that scores top marks for both novelty and feasibility, and because A can be finished by 4 January 2027 on free GPUs without a large volunteer annotation effort. We judge that either bundle, well executed, is a realistic Findings acceptance and a plausible main-conference paper, with the mechanistic or causal layer deciding which. All scores and effort figures below are estimates or judgments.

### 1.1 Low-compute papers win on sharp questions, and Vietnamese work lacks depth rather than coverage

**None of the three ACL 2026 Best Papers depends on large-scale compute**: they are a diagnostic dataset with representational analysis, a formal-theory paper and a cognitive-modeling study whose model scale we did not check ([ACL 2026 best papers](https://2026.aclweb.org/program/best_papers/)). Accepted low-compute genres include behavioral analyses such as the ACL 2025 Best Paper on response sampling ([2025.acl-long.1454](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1454/)) and benchmarks built or verified by native speakers, such as Javanese honorifics (ACL 2025) and BanglaSocialBench, an ACL 2026 Student Research Workshop Best Paper ([2025.acl-long.1296](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1296/); [2026.acl-srw.22](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-srw.22/)). They also include minimal-pair suites such as TurBLiMP ([2025.emnlp-main.834](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.834/)), character-knowledge tests such as CUTE and EXECUTE ([2024.emnlp-main.177](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.177/); [2025.findings-acl.95](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.95/)), and interpretability on small open models, including an EMNLP 2025 Outstanding Paper run on **two A40 GPUs** ([2025.emnlp-main.1271](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1271/)). The lesson for both bundles (our judgment): **a benchmark alone tends to land in Findings or a workshop, while main-conference and award papers pair new data with a mechanistic or causal analysis**.

The bar is rising. ACL 2026 accepted **18.9% of 12,148 submissions to the main conference and 17.8% to Findings** ([ACL 2026 front matter](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.0.pdf)), and by our regex count "benchmark" appears in **47% of 2026 ACL and EACL titles and abstracts, up from 27% across the major venues in 2024** ([Anthology data](https://github.com/acl-org/acl-anthology/tree/master/data/xml)). We judge several Vietnamese directions saturated: knowledge suites such as VMLU ([2025.acl-long.563](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.563/)), translated English benchmarks, dialect robustness, taken by VialectBench in August 2026 ([arXiv 2608.10414](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.10414)), idioms, language-identity features ([2025.acl-long.229](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.229/)) and training new tokenizers ([arXiv 2609.21362](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21362)). Open directions, each with accepted analogs in other languages, include Southeast Asian social deixis, a Vietnamese minimal-pair suite, sub-syllable tokenization analyses and single-phenomenon Gemma Scope 2 studies.

What Vietnamese lacks is depth. Only **4–7 papers a year (0.05–0.13%)** had "Vietnamese" in the title in the ACL, EMNLP, NAACL and EACL main and Findings volumes of 2024–2026 ([Anthology data](https://github.com/acl-org/acl-anthology/tree/master/data/xml)), and existing Vietnamese suites test tasks and knowledge rather than isolating a mechanism of the language (our reading). SEA-HELM's linguistic diagnostics, as first published, cover only Indonesian and Tamil ([2025.findings-acl.636](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.636/)), MultiBLiMP excludes Vietnamese ([arXiv 2504.02768](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.02768)), EXECUTE has none, and we found no LLM study of kinship-based address (xưng hô), classifiers, reduplication, Sino-Vietnamese vocabulary or nói lái. ARR's reviewer guidelines already list "Only [non-English] language" and "The topic is too niche" as problematic reviewing heuristics ([ARR reviewer guidelines](https://aclrollingreview.org/reviewerguidelines)), so a Vietnamese-only paper is procedurally safe; its real risk is reading as a clone of another language's benchmark.

### 1.3 Bundle A is the default; switching to B takes a failed pilot and committed annotators

**Bundle A** has three parts. A rule engine generates and verifies nói lái, the folk wordplay that swaps rhymes and/or tones between syllables ("mèo cái → mài kéo", "bí mật → bị mất") in four documented variants ([Vietnamese Language Studies](https://vlstudies.wordpress.com/2016/12/08/noi-lai-vietnamese-spoonerism/)). It enforces spelling rules (c/k/q, g/gh, ng/ngh, tone-mark placement), yields forward-swap, decoding and validity items beside 200–400 attested folk and literary examples, and relates accuracy to per-syllable token counts across about ten tokenizers. Re-encoding experiments rewrite existing Vietnamese benchmark items with meaning held fixed, switching diacritics between precomposed and combining Unicode forms (NFC versus NFD) and between old and new tone-mark placement, with a mediation analysis through token count. In weeks 8–9, probing Gemma 3 1B and 4B locates where tone and rhyme information is reconstructed, the layer we expect to lift the paper toward the main conference.

A is primary because it is the cheapest, least volunteer-dependent and fully rule-scored option, and even its failure modes yield findings. If production tokenizers normalize to NFC, "which tokenizers normalize?" becomes the result, while tone-placement variants survive normalization; if reasoning models solve nói lái step by step, the result becomes accuracy against reasoning-token cost. Its weakness is an acceptance score of 3: wordplay alone invites a "toy or niche" critique that the merger and the probe must answer.

**Bundle B** pairs XưngHô-Bench with a mechanism section. Vietnamese speakers refer to themselves and their addressee with paired terms such as anh–em, cháu–bác or tôi–bạn, chosen by relative age, kinship, gender, setting, region and stance. The benchmark tests choosing the right pair, keeping it across turns, interpreting a switch and translating English "I/you" into a pair; the mechanism section seeks Gemma 3 1B and 4B features for seniority, gender and intimacy, then patches or steers them to see whether pronoun choice flips.

Our switching rule builds on the plan's week-2 go/no-go checks: **stay with A unless, by Gate 1 on 18 October 2026, A's premise has failed and B's precondition holds**. A's premise fails if a three-model pilot on 200 items per idea shows no measurable effect, or if the novelty sweep finds a preprint already testing nói lái or Vietnamese re-encoding on LLMs. B's precondition is six to eight native annotators, balanced across North, Centre and South, committed in week 1. Annotators alone are no reason to switch: they are better spent validating A's items and supplying its human baseline, and B's precedent does not offset its higher scoop risk or its exposure to volunteer delays in the weeks when application deadlines cluster. If Gemma 3 4B cannot do the Vietnamese task above chance, B's mechanism section goes and B shrinks to a benchmark most likely bound for Findings. If A fails and no annotators appear, our suggested fallback is a short paper on the Sino-Vietnamese pilot, if its signal is strong.

### 1.4 Bundle A's novelty case: nói lái and meaning-preserving re-encoding are unstudied in LLMs

A joins an active literature on what LLMs know about the characters inside their tokens, but the multilingual benchmark in that line, EXECUTE, covers ten languages and not Vietnamese ([2025.findings-acl.95](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.95/)). "Spelling-out is not Straightforward" shows that models reconstruct character information in intermediate layers and is the template for A's probe ([2025.findings-emnlp.719](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.719/)). An ACL 2026 study of how tokenization limits phonological knowledge introduces the STAD syllabification-alignment metric ([2026.acl-long.634](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.634/)); we did not check which languages it covers, so it must be read before A's framing is fixed. The closest causal design, "Causal Estimation of Tokenisation Bias", trained its own 57M–850M-parameter models ([2025.acl-long.1374](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1374/)), and the closest re-encoding designs concern Japanese tokenization inconsistency and Arabic diacritics ([2025.acl-short.75](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-short.75/); [2026.findings-eacl.22](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-eacl.22/)). For Vietnamese, diacritic removal appears only as one of five typo perturbations in the "Crossing Linguistic Horizons" evaluation ([2024.findings-naacl.182](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-naacl.182/)), and the only study we found of how neural models represent Vietnamese tone concerns speech models ([2024.naacl-long.239](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.239/)).

**The exact gap: we found no LLM paper that uses nói lái or any Vietnamese spoonerism, and none that compares NFC with NFD or old with new tone placement**, although Vietnamese diacritics "may be encoded either as combining characters or as precomposed characters" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_language_and_computers)). That is a natural experiment in which meaning is fixed and only the tokens change. The paper should lead with a general question: can subword LLMs reach the initial consonant, rhyme and tone hidden inside the tokens of a tonal script written syllable by syllable, and does failure grow with fragmentation? Nói lái is the instrument, not the headline; the re-encodings are a training-free intervention on deployed tokenizers; the probe extends "Spelling-out" from characters to tone. The paper must also separate itself from two tokenizer-building preprints. "Beyond Atomic Tokens" (18 September 2026) pretrains PhonemicBERT models on Vietnamese and Mandarin syllables factorized into onset, rime and tone ([arXiv 2609.21362](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21362)), and "Equity with Efficiency" (June 2026) trained 1.5B-parameter models on 4–8 H100/H200 GPUs to compare tokenizers across 11 Southeast Asian languages ([arXiv 2606.15044](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15044)). They change the tokenizer while A diagnoses deployed ones, and A's optional syllable-factorized or IPA input condition would absorb the competing idea as a baseline (our judgment).

Both halves score 4 of 5 for scoop safety, yet VialectBench and the syllable tokenizer each appeared within two months of our check. We judge the likeliest overlaps to be an LLM-evaluation follow-up from the tokenizer's authors or an EXECUTE-style suite extended to Vietnamese. arXiv search was blocked and Semantic Scholar rate-limited during the check, so the week 1–2 sweep for nói lái, spoonerism, Vietnamese tokenization, NFC/NFD and tone placement is a genuine gate, and a Google Scholar alert should run until submission. A rival posted after early October is partly cushioned: ARR treats papers appearing less than three months before the deadline as contemporaneous and does not require detailed comparison with them ([ARR CFP](https://aclrollingreview.org/cfp)).

### 1.5 Bundle B's novelty case: Vietnamese address appears only as a solved binary formality task

The one overlapping resource is the IWSLT 2023 English→Vietnamese formality track, which reduces Vietnamese address to formal versus informal ([IWSLT 2023 formality track](https://iwslt.org/2023/formality)). By our count of its released test data, **"quý vị" appears in 93.3% of formal references, "bạn" in 97.7% of informal ones and any kinship-term marker in only 4.2% of pairs** ([IWSLT 2023 data](https://github.com/amazon-science/contrastive-controlled-mt)), and the best reported system reaches **99.4% (formal) and 98.8% (informal)** matched accuracy ([2023.iwslt-1.40](https://aclanthology.org/2023.iwslt-1.40.pdf)). The nearest LLM benchmarks skip Vietnamese: BanglaSocialBench (1,719 items; models default to over-formal address and err most when an elder addresses someone younger), Javanese Unggah-Ungguh, Korean honorific translation and Hindi/Bengali honorifics ([2026.acl-srw.22](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-srw.22/); [2025.acl-long.1296](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1296/); [2026.iaai-1.3](https://aclanthology.org/2026.iaai-1.3/); [2025.emnlp-main.966](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.966/)). Multilingual sparse-autoencoder studies target language identity and grammatical concepts ([2025.acl-long.229](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.229/); [2025.naacl-long.312](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.312/)), and apart from a layer-wise probe of politeness in a Japanese BERT ([2026.socon-1.7](https://aclanthology.org/2026.socon-1.7/)), we found no interpretability study of politeness, honorifics or address, and none that tests such features causally. Gemma Scope 2 covers every Gemma 3 size from 270M to 27B ([Google DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/blog/gemma-scope-2-helping-the-ai-safety-community-deepen-understanding-of-complex-language-model-behavior/)), so such a study fits one free T4 at the 1B–4B sizes (our estimate).

The remaining gap has five parts. No Vietnamese dialogue resource annotates speaker relationships with the valid self/addressee pairs. No metric scores reciprocity, so a model that calls its addressee "chú" (uncle) but itself "tôi" instead of "cháu" passes every formality test. No study checks whether models keep a Vietnamese pair consistent across turns or switch it when a relationship changes (a revealed age, romance, anger with tao/mày, deference), none tests reading age, gender or relation off address terms, and none covers Northern versus Southern variants. The framing that stays novel is **the first evaluation of the reciprocal, relationship-indexed Vietnamese address system, including its dynamics across turns**, scored with lexicon-based checks of pair validity, reciprocity, consistency and switch accuracy. It should be grounded in Luong's account of Vietnamese person reference ([Benjamins](https://benjamins.com/catalog/pbns.11)) and built on the contrastive design of Voita et al.'s Russian formal/informal "you" test set ([P19-1116](https://aclanthology.org/P19-1116.pdf)). Its anchor experiment should test whether near-ceiling IWSLT-style formality accuracy coexists with poor reciprocal kinship choice; without reciprocity, dynamics and comprehension clearly in scope, reviewers are likely to see an IWSLT extension or a "Vietnamese BanglaSocialBench".

B's scoop safety is only 3 of 5. In September 2026, a Hindi, Tamil and Korean kinship-term benchmark found GPT-OSS-120B recognizing the correct term in **90.67%** of test cells but producing an accepted one in only **36.00%** ([arXiv 2609.26942](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.26942)), and a KAIST preprint evaluated register, honorifics and cross-turn consistency in machine-translated conversation for Arabic, Bengali, Indonesian and Korean ([arXiv 2609.19885](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.19885)). Vietnamese is an obvious next language for either group (our judgment), and EMNLP and AACL 2026 papers were not yet indexed when we searched, so B needs its own Scholar alert pairing "Vietnamese" with pronoun, xưng hô, address term or kinship.

### 1.6 Both bundles fit standard areas, but only B can credibly enter the ACL 2027 theme

The ACL 2027 call, published 29 September 2026, lists 29 areas plus a theme track, **"Homogenization and knowledge collapse in LLMs"**, with an anticipated special session and Thematic Paper Award ([ACL 2027 call](https://2027.aclweb.org/calls/main/)). Relevant areas include "Multilingualism and Cross-Lingual NLP", "Language Diversity and NLP for Low-resourced Languages" and "Interpretability and Analysis of Models for NLP"; the call omits "Resources and Evaluation", which ARR still lists, and ARR's submission form, where authors pick one area, is what governs ([ACL 2027 call source](https://github.com/acl-org/acl-2027/blob/main/_pages/calls/main.md); [ARR areas](https://aclrollingreview.org/areas)). We place **Bundle A** in "Multilingualism and Cross-Lingual NLP", ticking the "Data resources", "Data analysis" and "Model analysis & interpretability" contribution types ([ARR CFP](https://aclrollingreview.org/cfp)), and would move it to "Interpretability and Analysis of Models for NLP" if the causal and probing results carry the paper. In our judgment A cannot credibly target the theme, which concerns the uniformity of model outputs rather than how inputs are represented.

**Bundle B** belongs in "Resources and Evaluation" if the January form lists it and in "Multilingualism and Cross-Lingual NLP" otherwise, and it alone fits the theme, whose cross-linguistic subtopic asks whether LLMs "respond more uniformly than humans would" and adapt "to the writing and cultural norms that best associate to the query language". B's core hypothesis, the Vietnamese counterpart of BanglaSocialBench's over-formal default, is that models collapse a relationship-driven system onto a neutral tôi/bạn pair or onto one region's forms, which in our judgment is cultural flattening in the theme's exact sense; comparing the diversity of model and human pair choices would answer the theme's question directly. The theme decision should wait until the pilot shows the collapse. Theme tracks accepted 69% of submissions to main or Findings at ACL 2024 (38 of 55) and 45% at ACL 2026 (56 of 124), against roughly 43% and 37% of all submissions (our calculation), though such small, self-selected samples do not prove an easier bar ([ACL 2024 front matter](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.0.pdf); [ACL 2026 front matter](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.0.pdf)).

## 2. The paper blueprint

Bundle A is specified close to submission level and Bundle B in compact form. Results appear only as **labelled hypotheses or bracketed placeholders**, every Vietnamese example is **to be verified by a native speaker**, and any specific without a source is a design choice. The template is the pattern behind main-track low-compute papers: a new dataset joined to a causal analysis. The EMNLP 2025 Outstanding filler-gap paper followed it, running Distributed Alignment Search over templated minimal pairs on Pythia models of at most 6.9B parameters ([2025.emnlp-main.1271](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1271/)).

### 2.1 Bundle A sells a tokenization finding, with wordplay as the instrument

Three working titles fit: *Tones Hidden in Tokens: How LLMs Handle Vietnamese Sub-Syllabic Structure*; *Mèo Cái or Mài Kéo? A Spoonerism Benchmark and Orthographic Counterfactuals for Vietnamese*; and *Same Syllable, Different Tokens: Causal Effects of Orthography on LLMs in a Tonal Script*. The first leads with the research question and suits the ARR areas "Multilingualism and Cross-Lingual NLP" and "Interpretability and Analysis of Models for NLP" ([ARR CFP](https://aclrollingreview.org/cfp)). The second is catchier but reads as a dataset paper. Draft abstract:

> Written Vietnamese encodes each syllable's onset, rime and tone, yet subword tokenizers split syllables without regard to this structure. Can large language models manipulate units they never receive as tokens? We introduce NóiLái, a benchmark built on nói lái, a native Vietnamese wordplay that swaps rimes, onsets or tones between syllables. A rule engine encoding Vietnamese spelling generates [≈10,000] automatically verifiable items across three tasks (transformation, decoding, validity judgment) and four documented variants, plus [200–400] attested folk and literary examples; native speakers validated a stratified sample [α = PLACEHOLDER]. Across [17] self-hosted and [4] API-served models, [H1: accuracy tracks how well token boundaries align with onset and rime boundaries more than raw token count; best model PLACEHOLDER vs. humans PLACEHOLDER]. We then intervene on tokenization with meaning held fixed, re-encoding text between precomposed and combining diacritics and between tone-mark placement conventions. We estimate the causal effects on NóiLái and on an existing Vietnamese benchmark, including the share that runs through token count [H3: PLACEHOLDER]. Probes and activation patching in Gemma 3 1B and 4B locate [H5: where tone becomes linearly decodable, PLACEHOLDER]. We release the generator, syllable parser and re-encoding toolkit.

The introduction should claim five contributions:

1. **NóiLái**, the first Vietnamese benchmark for manipulating units inside the syllable: a rule-verified generator, three tasks over four variants, an attested cultural subset, native validation and a human baseline.
2. **A syllable-alignment audit** linking how production tokenizers split onset, rime and tone to item-level accuracy.
3. **Orthographic counterfactuals** that change only the token sequence, separating tokenizer-caused from knowledge-caused errors, with a census of which tokenizers normalize Unicode.
4. **A mechanistic localization** of tone and rime information in small open models, using control-validated probes and activation patching.
5. **Reusable tooling** (parser, spelling-rule engine, re-encoder) for other diacritic-heavy writing systems.

A late-September 2026 search of the ACL Anthology and the web found the area open, though arXiv could not be searched directly. EXECUTE's character-knowledge tests exclude Vietnamese ([2025.findings-acl.95](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.95/)). The nearest analyses show models rebuilding character information in intermediate layers ([2025.findings-emnlp.719](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.719/)) and tokenization limiting phonological knowledge ([2026.acl-long.634](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.634/)). Vietnamese diacritic removal appears only as one of five typo perturbations in an existing evaluation ([2024.findings-naacl.182](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-naacl.182/)), and none of 29 humor and pun titles from 2024–2026 is Vietnamese ([Anthology data](https://github.com/acl-org/acl-anthology/tree/master/data/xml)). The closest causal designs study Arabic diacritics ([2026.findings-eacl.22](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-eacl.22/)) or train 57M–850M-parameter models ([2025.acl-long.1374](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1374/)), whereas re-encoding the inputs of fixed models needs no training. A September 2026 preprint trains new models with an onset/rime/tone tokenizer ([arXiv 2609.21362](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21362)). It complements rather than pre-empts a diagnosis of existing models, so cite it and repeat the arXiv sweep before submission.

### 2.2 Three tasks and three re-encoding arms turn wordplay into minimal pairs

Nói lái has four documented variants ([Vietnamese Language Studies](https://vlstudies.wordpress.com/2016/12/08/noi-lai-vietnamese-spoonerism/)). That source is a blog, and no reference on tone-mark placement has been checked, so a published grammar must anchor the rule tables before any data are built. Nguyễn Đình-Hoà's reference grammar is a candidate, though its coverage of placement is unchecked ([John Benjamins](https://benjamins.com/catalog/loall.9)).

| Variant | Swapped between the two syllables | Kept in place |
|---|---|---|
| **V1** | rimes | onsets, tones |
| **V2** | onset plus rime | tones |
| **V3** ("Northern style") | tones only | onsets, rimes |
| **V4** | rimes and tones | onsets |

**T1 (transformation)** maps a two-syllable input and a named variant to its output. **T2 (decoding)** asks what meaningful phrase an input hides. Each variant undoes itself when applied twice, so naming it would collapse T2 into T1. T2 therefore withholds the variant and accepts any (variant, output) pair whose output natives judge to be real Vietnamese. **T3 (validity)** pairs each correct output with invalid twins that each differ in one component type (onset, rime, tone or spelling). This is the contrastive logic of BLiMP ([TACL](https://aclanthology.org/2020.tacl-1.25/)) and of Voita et al.'s symmetric sets, on which a model that ignores context scores 50% ([P19-1116](https://aclanthology.org/P19-1116.pdf)). Three arms then re-encode inputs. **C1** swaps precomposed and combining diacritics (NFC/NFD), both valid encodings of Vietnamese ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_language_and_computers)). **C2** swaps the two tone-mark placement conventions. **C3** strips accents, a contrast arm that destroys information rather than preserving it. **P** items feed the probes.

| T1, V3 | hoạ sĩ | hoã sị ≡ hõa sị (either placement) | "painter" → pseudo-phrase | to be verified by a native speaker |
| T2 | Đầu tiên | {(V2, tiền đâu)} | "first" → "where's the money?"; attested | to be verified by a native speaker |

### 2.3 A rule engine builds 10,000 items and three volunteers validate 1,000

Base pairs come from real two-syllable words, segmented with VnCoreNLP ([N18-5012](https://aclanthology.org/N18-5012/)) from openly licensed text, and from pseudo-pairs sampled from legal onset–rime–tone combinations. Items are stratified by onset presence, glide rimes such as "oa", spelling triggers (c/k/q, g/gh, ng/ngh), tone pair and whether the output is a real word. Pair–variant combinations that return the input unchanged or yield an illegal syllable are dropped. The pool holds **about 10,000 items plus 200–400 attested examples**. It is split **by base pair** into a public dev set (15–20%) and a gated test set, with a balanced **1,500-item core** (125 per task × variant cell) for the API models.

Controls keep the benchmark measuring "the phenomenon and only the phenomenon" ([Bean et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04703)). Lexicality and syllable frequency enter every regression, T3 is balanced 50/50 with randomized answer order, and a name-only prompt separates cultural recall from rule-following. Contamination is a weak threat because a generator can regenerate its test set. The dev split ships under CC BY 4.0, and the test split is gated under CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 with a canary string ([2023.emnlp-main.308](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.308/); [BIG-bench](https://github.com/google/BIG-bench/blob/main/docs/doc.md)). Gemini's free tier uses submitted content "to improve our products" ([Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)), so APIs see only the core, and a sealed, regenerated set never reaches any API. A guided-instruction test measures contamination in the attested subset ([Golchin & Surdeanu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08493)). The C arms also run on XCOPA's 500 Vietnamese test items (CC-BY-4.0; [XCOPA](https://github.com/cambridgeltl/xcopa)). Memorized spellings could inflate effects there, so each effect is re-estimated on fresh NóiLái items.

The author builds the engine, curates the attested set and settles disputes. Three adult native validators judge a stratified 1,000 items for correctness, spelling, lexicality and offensive readings, ideally including Northern and Southern speakers since V3 is described as Northern. Each item gets two validators and a 200-item overlap gets all three, meeting the three-coder convention for Krippendorff's α ([Artstein & Poesio](https://aclanthology.org/J08-4004.pdf)). A confirmed generator bug triggers a fix and full regeneration, and generator precision is reported with a confidence interval. About 20 natives spend roughly 20 minutes each on a human-baseline form, keeping volunteer time near **20–40 hours**.

### 2.4 Code scores every item, and paired tests carry every comparison

No primary metric needs an LLM judge, which matters because multilingual judges averaged a Fleiss' κ of only about 0.3 across 25 languages ([Fu & Liu](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.587/)). T1 scores exact match after normalizing Unicode and tone-mark placement. Parsing outputs into onset, rime and tone adds per-component accuracy and illegal, copy and unparseable rates, an error taxonomy at no annotation cost. T2 scores gold-set membership and variant identification. T3 scores generated answers for every model, since lm-eval's chat-API backends return no log-probabilities ([lm-evaluation-harness](https://github.com/EleutherAI/lm-evaluation-harness)). Open models add answer-token probabilities, as Hu et al. scored answers ([2023.acl-long.230](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.230.pdf)), and per-token string log-probabilities, because prompted judgments understate what probabilities reveal ([Hu & Levy](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.306/)). Tokenizers are profiled by tokens per syllable, the share of single-token syllables, diacritic splits, NFD normalization and boundary alignment. Boundary alignment is the share of token boundaries that fall on syllable or onset–rime boundaries, after the ACL 2026 STAD metric.

Rigor is cheap differentiation: only **16.0% of 445 reviewed LLM benchmarks** reported uncertainty estimates or statistical tests ([Bean et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04703)). Headline accuracies carry 95% bootstrap intervals that resample whole base pairs ([Miller](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640)). Model comparisons and interventions use McNemar ([Dror et al.](https://aclanthology.org/P18-1128.pdf)) or a paired bootstrap, Holm-corrected within each model × intervention family ([Dror et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/html/1709.09500)). A mixed-effects logistic regression with random intercepts for base pair and model estimates fragmentation effects. A mediation analysis estimates the share of each intervention effect carried by token count, under a stated no-unmeasured-confounding assumption. Results span 3–5 native-written paraphrases, since formatting alone moved one model's accuracy by up to 76 points ([Sclar et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11324)). By normal approximation, 1,000 items at 70% accuracy give about **±2.8 points**, and 94% power for a 5-point paired difference when 20% of pairs are discordant. The 125-item API cells resolve only contrasts of roughly 10 points, so cell-level claims rest on open models. Metrics and analyses are pre-registered in a dated commit.

### 2.6 Experiments escalate from correlation to intervention to mechanism

A week-2 pilot of three open models on 200 items per idea is the go/no-go gate. **E1** then runs all 21 models against human, random and copy baselines. **E2** asks what predicts failure. The count account says more tokens per syllable hurt, because onset, rime and tone vanish inside opaque tokens. **H1** says alignment matters more, so byte fragments that cross onset–rime boundaries hurt even at low counts. Within one model some syllables are single tokens and others are split, which identifies the effect without cross-model confounds once frequency is controlled. Comparing SEA-LION v4.5 E2B with Gemma 4 E2B isolates SEA training, if the fine-tune kept its base tokenizer (to verify). **H2** predicts that PhoGPT's vocabulary aids fluency but hurts manipulation, if it stores whole syllables as tokens.

**E3** runs C1–C3 on NóiLái and Vietnamese XCOPA. **H3** predicts a crossover that token count alone cannot produce: NFD helps tone-sensitive V3 items by making tone an explicit symbol, but hurts XCOPA through longer, rarer sequences. Tokenizers that normalize to NFC show zero effect by construction. Placement variants survive normalization, so **H4** predicts small nonzero C2 shifts in every model.

**E4** applies the "Spelling-out is not Straightforward" template to Gemma 3 1B and 4B. Layer-wise linear probes at each syllable's final sub-token decode tone, onset and rime under NFC and NFD, trained and tested on disjoint syllables against random-label control tasks ([Hewitt & Liang](https://aclanthology.org/D19-1275/)). Probes show that information is present, not that it is used ([Belinkov](https://aclanthology.org/2022.cl-1.7/)). Activation patching on P pairs therefore locates where transplanted states flip the output tone, and a difference-in-means direction, CausalGym's baseline ([2024.acl-long.785](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.785/)), tests steering. Gemma Scope 2 residual SAEs ([HF](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-scope-2-4b-it)) are optional and browsable free on Neuronpedia, but must beat a probe baseline ([Kantamneni et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/kantamneni25a.html)). **H5** predicts a dissociation: tone is decodable by the middle layers even in models that fail V3, which would place the failure in manipulation rather than perception. A clean localization would be equally publishable.

Ablations vary variant, lexicality, spelling trigger, instruction language, number of demonstrations, thinking mode and input format (raw, character-spaced, or explicit onset–rime–tone). Near-ceiling accuracy on explicit input would pin the bottleneck on sub-token access rather than rule logic. **H6** predicts that attested items far outscore matched generated ones, the signature of memorization.

### 2.8 Bundle B trades the tokenizer story for sociopragmatics and 150–250 annotator-hours

The lead title is *Reciprocity Is Not Formality: XưngHô-Bench and the Mechanics of Vietnamese Address in LLMs*, with *Anh or Em? How LLMs Choose Vietnamese Kinship Address* as fallback. Draft abstract:

> Vietnamese speakers refer to themselves and their addressees with reciprocal pairs indexed to their relationship (anh–em, cháu–bác, con–mẹ), yet the only prior Vietnamese formality task reduces the system to a binary bạn/quý vị switch that systems already solve. We introduce XưngHô-Bench, [≈1,200] native-written dialogue items annotated for relative age, gender, relationship, setting and region, with set-valued, region-tagged gold. Tasks cover pair selection, multi-turn consistency under scripted relationship change, switch interpretation and EN→VI translation. Reciprocity-aware metrics show [H: models default to neutral tôi/bạn, err most in elder-to-younger speech, and produce invalid pairs despite near-ceiling formality accuracy; PLACEHOLDER]. In Gemma 3 [1B/4B], relative age is [H: linearly decodable by layer PLACEHOLDER], and steering along it [H: flips anh to em in PLACEHOLDER% of items].

The first three contributions follow the novelty verdict, and the fourth adds the mechanism study:

1. The first relationship-annotated benchmark for Vietnamese person reference.
2. A reciprocity-aware lexicon and metrics validated against native speakers.
3. A diagnostic showing that the formality ceiling does not transfer to kinship address.
4. A mechanism section on small Gemma 3 models.

The diagnostic rests on hard numbers. In the IWSLT 2023 EN→VI data, "quý vị" appears in **93.3%** of formal test references, "bạn" in **97.7%** of informal ones, and kinship markers in only **4.2%** of test pairs ([data repo](https://github.com/amazon-science/contrastive-controlled-mt)). Yet the best system reached 99.4% and 98.8% matched accuracy ([2023.iwslt-1.40](https://aclanthology.org/2023.iwslt-1.40.pdf)). Analogous benchmarks predict the failure modes. BanglaSocialBench's 1,719 items concentrated errors in Elder→Younger contexts ([2026.acl-srw.22](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-srw.22/)), and GPT-OSS-120B recognized the correct Hindi, Tamil or Korean kinship term in 90.67% of cells but produced an accepted one in only 36.00% ([arXiv 2609.26942](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.26942)).

The four tasks are pair selection, regex-scored multi-turn consistency, switch interpretation and EN→VI dialogue translation, drawn from 150–250 hand-written base scenarios. Each scenario expands into 4–6 variants that change one cue at a time (relative age, age gap, gender, relationship, setting, region or turn distance), yielding 1,000–1,500 items with 15–20% held out as dev. Answer-preserving perturbations, cue-removed controls and logged writer IDs guard against artifacts. For example, a 30-year-old woman asking a 60-year-old male neighbour for directions takes the self-term {cháu} and the addressee term {bác, chú}. Calling him "chú" while calling herself "tôi" or "em" breaks reciprocity yet passes any formality metric (to be verified by a native speaker).

Annotators mark each candidate in a closed inventory as acceptable, marginal or unacceptable, then give a preferred term and their region. Gold is the majority-acceptable set, marginal terms earn half credit reported separately, and regional variants are tagged rather than forced into one answer ([Plank](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.02570)). A 50-item, three-annotator pilot precedes the main round, in which every item gets at least two validators and a 200-item overlap at least three. Agreement is reported as nominal Krippendorff's α over per-candidate decisions, α on the acceptable sets under both MASI ([L06-1392](https://aclanthology.org/L06-1392/)) and Jaccard distance, and α on the preferred term, all bootstrapped and computable in NLTK ([NLTK](https://www.nltk.org/api/nltk.metrics.agreement.html)). Both distances matter, because the choice alone moved α from 0.642 to 0.691 on the same data; by convention, α above .8 is good and .67–.8 tentative ([Artstein & Poesio](https://aclanthology.org/J08-4004.pdf)).

The metrics are self- and addressee-term set accuracy, pair validity against a hand-built reciprocal lexicon, consistency@k across turns, switch accuracy at scripted change points, a separately counted avoidance rate (tôi/bạn, dropped pronouns or English), contrastive log-probability accuracy for open models, and region-matched accuracy. Two natives validate the term extractor on 100 outputs per model, as in WinoMT, where automatic–human agreement averaged 87% against 90% between humans ([P19-1164](https://aclanthology.org/P19-1164.pdf)).

The analysis layer adds a double-coded error taxonomy, cue-ablation regressions and Gemma 3 scaling from 270M to 27B. Its core is a mechanism study on Gemma 3 1B and 4B that uses dialogues differing only in relative age and targets logit(anh) − logit(em) at the address slot. It combines cross-template probes and control tasks with activation patching, difference-in-means steering and optional Gemma Scope 2 SAEs. TalkTuner is the precedent: its probes decoded users' age, gender, education and socioeconomic status at 0.91–0.98 accuracy, and steering changed responses on 93–100% of test questions ([TalkTuner](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07882v2)). A week-2 gate requires Gemma 3 4B to beat chance on pair selection. Compute stays modest, an estimated 5–15 GPU-hours for evaluation and 20–60 for the mechanism study, so people are the binding cost. Six to eight natives balanced across North, Central and South need about 100–150 hours of writing and 40–60 of cross-validation, and a 20–30-person human baseline adds about 30 minutes each. That comes to **150–250 annotator-hours** in all, plus full checklist Part D paperwork.

Three terms matter more than the quotas. First, **some free tiers train on their inputs**. vLLM needs compute capability 7.5, which the T4 meets and the older P100 does not ([vLLM](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/getting_started/installation/gpu.html)), so 2×T4 is the default and the P100 only a llama.cpp fallback. On the T4, vLLM runs without FlashAttention-2, bf16 or FP8 but supports AWQ, GPTQ, bitsandbytes and GGUF ([Bear the Tokens](https://www.dhruvvakharwala.dev/blogs/bear-the-tokens-qwen-t4-throughput); [vLLM docs](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/main/docs/features/quantization/README.md)). The recipe is one pinned vLLM version, fp16, Triton attention, memory utilization at or below 0.9, and CUDA graphs on unless a model forces eager mode, since disabling them roughly halved throughput in one T4 study ([Bear the Tokens](https://www.dhruvvakharwala.dev/blogs/bear-the-tokens-qwen-t4-throughput); [kaggle-vllm](https://github.com/kaggle-vllm/kaggle-vllm)). A smoke test of each model family comes first. Gemma 3 overflows in fp16, and even a clamping fix cut one benchmark average from 0.752 to 0.657 ([transformers #36832](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/pull/36832)); Gemma 4 E2B needed a kernel patch and 16 GB of swap to load on a T4 ([dev.to](https://dev.to/gde/gemma-4-on-a-tesla-t4-qat-weights-decode-179x-faster-than-bf16-2fi4)); no source confirms that the Gated DeltaNet Qwen3.5–3.8 models run correctly under vLLM on the T4, so llama.cpp and the TPU are fallbacks; and Qwen3.5 reasons step by step by default ([HF](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-9B)), inflating output roughly tenfold (an estimate) unless switched off. Each TPU-hosted model must also load under vLLM-TPU and return prompt log-probabilities before its full run.

Two measurements anchor throughput. Qwen2.5-1.5B on Kaggle's 2×T4 produced **949 output tokens/s on one T4 and 1,284 on both at batch 16** ([vllm-t4-lab](https://github.com/Placidoe/vllm-t4-lab)), and a 4-bit Gemma 4 E2B ran at 72 tokens/s on one stream and 216 across eight ([dev.to](https://dev.to/gde/gemma-4-on-a-tesla-t4-qat-weights-decode-179x-faster-than-bf16-2fi4)). The budget therefore assumes 150–400 output and 1,000–2,000 prompt tokens/s for an 8B 4-bit model on one T4, rates the week-2 pilot must confirm. It also assumes about 3,300 NóiLái prompts per model (3,000 generated across the three task types plus about 300 attested examples) under three templates, with reasoning off and at most 64 output tokens. The orthographic counterfactuals start from XCOPA's 500 CC BY 4.0 Vietnamese test items ([XCOPA](https://github.com/cambridgeltl/xcopa)) and grow toward 2,000 only from test sets whose licenses allow it; each item is rewritten as NFC, NFD, old-style tone placement and unaccented text (the one variant that changes meaning), then scored by log-likelihood. A 500-item sub-study runs with reasoning on, and the four API models see only the 1,500-item core, with a 300-item subset of it under each paraphrase.

### Deterministic scoring keeps the tool stack free

**lm-evaluation-harness 0.4.13** drives vLLM, Hugging Face, llama.cpp servers and OpenAI-compatible APIs, with three caveats: backends need extras such as `lm_eval[vllm]`, chat APIs return no log-probabilities, and vLLM "occasionally differs in output from Huggingface" ([lm-eval](https://github.com/EleutherAI/lm-evaluation-harness)). Open models are therefore scored by log-likelihood and API models by generated letter choices, reported separately, and Bundle A's primary metric needs no LLM judge because the verifier checks each output against the set of valid transformations. For tone probing, **Gemma Scope 2** supplies CC-BY-4.0 sparse autoencoders and transcoders for every layer of every Gemma 3 model ([DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/blog/gemma-scope-2-helping-the-ai-safety-community-deepen-understanding-of-complex-language-model-behavior/)), browsable on Neuronpedia at zero compute ([Neuronpedia](https://www.neuronpedia.org/gemma-scope-2)) and loadable in SAELens 6.51 ([SAELens](https://github.com/decoderesearch/SAELens)); TransformerLens 4.0 removed `HookedTransformer.from_pretrained`, so older tutorials break ([TransformerLens](https://github.com/TransformerLensOrg/TransformerLens)). For native-speaker checks, **Google Forms plus Sheets** covers the roughly 1,000-item validation and the human baseline, **Label Studio** 1.23.2 suits Bundle B's heavier annotation ([Label Studio](https://github.com/HumanSignal/label-studio)), and Argilla should be avoided because it loses data on free Hugging Face Spaces ([Argilla](https://docs.argilla.io/latest/getting_started/how-to-configure-argilla-on-huggingface/)). NLTK computes Krippendorff's α with set distances such as MASI ([NLTK](https://www.nltk.org/api/nltk.metrics.agreement.html)), a gated Hugging Face dataset protects the test split ([HF](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/datasets-gated)), and a log of GPU-hours and package versions kept from day one answers checklist items C1 and C4 ([ARR checklist](https://aclrollingreview.org/responsibleNLPresearch/)).

## 4. Rules, odds, risks and fallbacks

**Procedure, not science, is the likeliest reason this paper fails.** ACL 2027 takes papers only through ACL Rolling Review (ARR), whose relevant cycle closes on **January 4, 2027 AoE** ([ACL 2027 home](https://2027.aclweb.org/)). Since October 2026, ARR guarantees review only to submissions that bring a qualified "service contributor". The author cannot fill that role, so recruiting one is the critical path. The rules below are ARR's as published in late September 2026. Figures from 2024–2026 are precedent only, because ACL 2027's commitment dates, fees, Student Research Workshop (SRW) call and workshop list are unpublished.

### A single qualified reviewer decides whether the paper is read at all

The rule is blunt: "Submissions will only be guaranteed review if they contribute a qualified service contributor." One person can back **at most two submissions, which ARR equates to ten reviews**, so each paper costs its contributor about five. Papers without a contributor enter a lottery, and "Submissions not selected for review via lottery will be desk rejected" ([ARR CFP](https://aclrollingreview.org/cfp)).

Automatic qualification takes one of two profiles. The first is a PhD, postdoc or faculty post plus two papers in major computational-linguistics or related venues, where Findings counts. The second is doctoral-student status ("but not bachelor and master students") or an industry Master's, plus two such papers and three overall. The degree-free route needs two ARR cycles of mentored secondary reviewing ([ARR qualifications](https://aclrollingreview.org/qualifications)). Only the October 2026 cycle falls before the deadline.

ARR refuses "one 'free' submission for the new members of the community", and resubmissions need a contributor too ([ARR policy FAQ](https://aclrollingreview.org/sustainable-reviewing-2026)). A separate ARR page cited in the methodology notes still mentions exceptions for authors "new to the community" ([ARR workload page](https://aclrollingreview.org/reviewing-workload-requirement/)). The current policy contradicts it, so it should not be relied on.

Two legitimate routes remain. A qualified researcher who meets ACL's authorship criteria can join as co-author, since contributors "will typically be selected among the authors". Alternatively, a non-author who "must be familiar with the submission" can vouch that it is "ready for consideration for acceptance at a top-tier conference" ([ARR CFP](https://aclrollingreview.org/cfp)). Such a voucher may be qualified in a different track ([ARR policy FAQ](https://aclrollingreview.org/sustainable-reviewing-2026)).

Adding a name purely to supply the service would breach the rule that author lists include "all (and only)" substantial contributors. The list also freezes at submission ([ARR CFP](https://aclrollingreview.org/cfp)). **We judge the co-author route likelier to work.** Five reviews is a heavy favour to ask of an outsider. A PhD student who shapes the design or analysis, by contrast, earns authorship and a stake in the result.

The mechanics leave no slack. The contributor needs an OpenReview profile with "affiliation, ORCID, DBLP and ACL Anthology links". They must **complete a registration form within 48 hours of the deadline, about January 6 AoE**, and then deliver the reviews. A contributor hit by an emergency without "a qualified and willing substitute" sinks every paper they serve or co-author ([ARR CFP](https://aclrollingreview.org/cfp)).

ARR's FAQ offers a workaround. The form appears "once a submission naming that contributor is made", and "This happens even if the submission is not yet final". Because the site "normally opens 2 weeks before the deadline", a skeleton submission around December 21 lets the contributor register early ([ARR policy FAQ](https://aclrollingreview.org/sustainable-reviewing-2026)).

January-cycle reviews were due February 7 in 2026 ([ACL 2026 call](https://2026.aclweb.org/calls/main_conference_papers/)). The author can share that load as the contributor's declared secondary reviewer ([ARR reviewer guidelines](https://aclrollingreview.org/reviewerguidelines)). That also counts toward the two cycles needed to qualify for later venues.

**We judge early December the last safe date to have a contributor confirmed.** January is ACL's heavy cycle, with 10,518 submissions in 2026 ([ARR May 2026 cycle letter](https://aclrollingreview.org/may26-cycle-letter)). Many qualified researchers will spend both slots on their own papers. The rest will want a strong draft first, because "Systematic endorsement of spammy submissions will be subject to sanctions" ([ARR CFP](https://aclrollingreview.org/cfp)).

The lottery is no plan. ARR publishes no odds, and 38% of the roughly 17,000 May 2026 submissions came from authors with no publication record (as extracted, [ARR policy](https://aclrollingreview.org/sustainable-reviewing-2026)). In December the author should also check two things. One is whether the dates page's possibly stale line "ALL authors must register" still applies ([ARR dates](https://aclrollingreview.org/dates)). The other is whether the promised qualification checker has launched ([ARR changelog](https://aclrollingreview.org/changelog)).

### Profiles, anonymity, the checklist and page limits can each end a paper before review

**Every author** needs a complete OpenReview profile within a week of the deadline, about January 11, or the paper is desk rejected. The profile must list ORCID, affiliation history, emails, conflicts of interest and any DBLP or Anthology links ([ARR CFP](https://aclrollingreview.org/cfp)).

Profiles on public email addresses can take two weeks to activate ([OpenReview signup](https://docs.openreview.net/getting-started/creating-an-openreview-profile/signing-up-for-openreview)). Profiles listing only "Independent Researcher" are rejected ([OpenReview FAQ](https://docs.openreview.net/getting-started/frequently-asked-questions/i-am-an-independent-researcher-how-do-i-sign-up)). High-school users should select "High School Student", and those aged 13–17 need signed parental consent ([OpenReview guidance](https://docs.openreview.net/getting-started/creating-an-openreview-profile/information-for-high-school-students)). ORCID is free ([ORCID](https://info.orcid.org/researchers/)). The contributor belongs on the author's profile as a conflict of interest.

Submissions must hide names, affiliations and identifying self-citations. They may not link to download-tracking hosts such as Dropbox. Non-anonymous preprints are allowed, but anonymity earns "priority in acceptance decisions for borderline papers". The binding no-preprint option forbids posting until meta-reviews arrive, "under the penalty of desk rejection" ([ARR CFP](https://aclrollingreview.org/cfp)).

At ACL 2023, reviewers who had seen a preprint were 1.75 times likelier to give higher Soundness scores (as extracted, [ACL 2023 PC report](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.911.pdf)). For this author, though, a named preprint mainly advertises an unaffiliated high-school student. **Our judgment: stay anonymous.** The cost is that no named preprint can support university applications before meta-reviews, around March. ACL 2027's option to make the anonymized submission publicly visible still leaves a dated record ([ACL 2027 call](https://2027.aclweb.org/calls/main/)).

The Responsible NLP checklist can itself cause desk rejection when "incorrect, incomplete or misleading". Yet a justified "no" is "not grounds for rejection" ([ARR checklist](https://aclrollingreview.org/responsibleNLPresearch)). Item C1 asks for parameter counts, "total computational budget (e.g., GPU hours)" and hardware, which a low-compute paper should simply state. C3 asks for error bars. D1–D5 cover annotator instructions, recruitment, pay, consent, ethics approval and demographics. Without an institutional review board, D4 needs a reasoned "No" and may draw an ethics flag. In our judgment that burden is light for Bundle A's small validation sample and central for Bundle B's annotation.

A section titled **"Limitations" is mandatory**. It sits outside the page count and "should not introduce new methods, analysis, or results". Omitting it, exceeding 8 pages for a long paper or 4 for a short one, or altering the template means rejection without review.

Recent cycles also desk-rejected papers for single-column appendices, "related work exclusively in the appendices", "vertical space hacking" and "hallucitations". Papers that two chairs judged below "the expected quality bar" were rejected too, and such rejections "can happen at any time in the review cycle".

While under ARR review, a paper may not be under review at any journal or "refereed and archival" venue. Withdrawing more than 48 hours after the deadline bars resubmission until "the second subsequent ARR cycle". Resubmissions must link their predecessor "even if the previous version of the paper was desk rejected" ([ARR CFP](https://aclrollingreview.org/cfp)).

### AI help is allowed if disclosed, but one invented citation can sink an accepted paper

Generative AI tools "do not qualify for authorship". Their "use for writing or coding, as well as its scope, must be disclosed in the Responsible NLP Checklist", with details in the Acknowledgements and optionally in code READMEs. Grammar checkers and short-form input aids need no disclosure. Generated low-novelty text should be identified and shown to have been checked, and model-suggested ideas should also be disclosed. Authors remain "fully responsible for the correctness" of their methods, results and writing ([ARR CFP](https://aclrollingreview.org/cfp)). Checklist item E1 demands a justification for any "no" ([ARR checklist](https://aclrollingreview.org/responsibleNLPresearch)).

The ACL 2027 call still links the 2023 policy ([call source](https://github.com/acl-org/acl-2027/blob/main/_pages/calls/main.md)). ARR follows the newer Publication Ethics policy ([ACL Publication Ethics](https://www.aclweb.org/adminwiki/index.php/ACL_Policy_on_Publication_Ethics)), which is the safer standard to meet. We infer that the cleanest practice is a running AI-use log written into E1 at submission. The Acknowledgements note can then be added at camera-ready, so disclosure never breaks anonymity.

Enforcement bites hardest on references. At camera-ready, ACL 2026 desk-rejected **over 100 already-accepted papers** that cited non-existent literature. It stressed "an author's obligation to manually confirm the existence and relevance of every cited source" (as extracted, [ACL 2026 statement](https://2026.aclweb.org/acl_statement/)). EMNLP 2026 rejected those papers too ([EMNLP 2026 note](https://2026.emnlp.org/concerning-late-desk-rejections/)), and ARR is drafting a hallucination policy ([ARR changelog](https://aclrollingreview.org/changelog)). Prompt injections aimed at AI reviewers also mean desk rejection ([ARR CFP](https://aclrollingreview.org/cfp)).

Every reference should be checked by hand. Any LLM judge should be validated against human ratings, since reviewers flag "LLM-only evaluation without validation" ([ARR reviewer guidelines](https://aclrollingreview.org/reviewerguidelines)).

### Presenting virtually cost about $350 in 2026, owed before fee waivers were decided

ACL 2027 is hybrid, but "Papers without at least one presenting author registered by the early registration deadline may be subject to rejection". The call does not say whether this binds Findings papers ([ACL 2027 call](https://2027.aclweb.org/calls/main/)), and the fee pages are not yet live ([navigation.yml](https://github.com/acl-org/acl-2027/blob/main/_data/navigation.yml)).

At 2026 early rates, student presenters paid **$750 in person, $300 virtually or $175 for a virtual one-day workshop**, plus $50 for student membership (as extracted). One registration covered two papers ([ACL 2026 registration](https://2026.aclweb.org/registration/)), so a virtual main-conference paper cost about **$350**.

Help was thin. Virtual subsidies excluded presenting authors ([virtual subsidies](https://2026.aclweb.org/calls/virtual_subsidies/)). Diversity-and-inclusion (D&I) subsidies could waive registration and fund travel for authors "lacking other funding sources", but reimbursed travel only after in-person attendance ([D&I subsidies](https://2026.aclweb.org/calls/di_subsidies/)). Ten volunteer hours earned "full-time students" free registration but no travel money ([volunteers](https://2026.aclweb.org/calls/volunteers/)).

Worse, the May 11 author-registration deadline came before the volunteer decisions (May 22) and the D&I decisions (May 26). Nothing defines whether a high-school student counts as a student. The author should ask the chairs early, or have a funded co-author register as presenter.

The main and Findings rates are shares of all submissions, as each program committee counted them (EMNLP 2025 and ACL 2026 as extracted). The other columns are our arithmetic. Committed papers fare far better because weak papers rarely commit, so ARR scores are the real filter.

ARR's Overall Assessment scale labels 4 "Conference", 3.5 "Borderline Conference", 3 "Findings" and 2.5 "Borderline Findings". Findings turns on "soundness and reproducibility", while main-conference recommendations "may also consider novelty, impact and other factors" ([ARR review form](https://aclrollingreview.org/reviewform)). No thresholds are published. Our reading is that about 3.5 or above, with Soundness of at least 3, contends for main. A score of 3 to 3.5 is Findings territory, and 2.5 or below rarely survives.

ACL 2023's data back the weight on soundness. Each point of mean Soundness roughly doubled the odds of acceptance, and senior area chairs' recommendations predicted outcomes best. Long papers reached main at 23.5%, against 16.5% for short ones (as extracted, [ACL 2023 PC report](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.911.pdf)).

No venue reports acceptance by career stage, so what follows is **judgment**. Because review is double-blind, a co-author raises the odds through the paper, not the name.

**With a qualified co-author** who genuinely shapes the design and writing, a well-executed Bundle A or B paper should roughly match the pool's two-in-five chance of main or Findings. Findings is the likely outcome. **Solo, backed only by a voucher**, the same idea plausibly falls to one in four or one in three. Unclear contributions, thin related work, overscoped claims and missing statistics are exactly what experienced collaborators catch. **With no contributor**, either figure is multiplied by an unpublished and probably small lottery probability.

The 2027 theme asks how models adapt "to the writing and cultural norms that best associate to the query language" ([ACL 2027 call](https://2027.aclweb.org/calls/main/)). In our judgment, it could house a Bundle B finding that models flatten address terms, but not Bundle A.

### Reviewers are told to forgive niche topics and to punish unvalidated evaluation

ARR's guidelines tell reviewers that several stock complaints are not weaknesses in themselves. These include "not novel" without a reference (H3), no state-of-the-art result (H5), negative results (H6), simplicity (H7) and a niche topic (H9). Studying a single non-English language (H10) also qualifies, because "Monolingual work on any language is important". So do language errors (H11), demands for extra experiments or closed-model comparisons (H13, H14) and acknowledged limitations (H16). The guidelines even cite dismissing a resource paper as "mostly a description of the corpus" as bad reviewing.

What reviewers should flag instead is validity ([ARR reviewer guidelines](https://aclrollingreview.org/reviewerguidelines)). The flagged problems include LLM-only evaluation without validation (M1), reproducibility gaps (M2), undisclosed data-quality issues (M3) and unmotivated model choice (M4). Others are ethics and licensing problems (T1–T2), best-of-N prompting and weak baselines (R1), overscoped claims (R2–R4), missing significance tests (R5) and faulty citations (G5).

Area chairs are reminded that a resource for a language "that does not yet have resources of that type may not sound very novel or exciting" yet can still be significant ([ARR AC guidelines](https://aclrollingreview.org/acguidelines)). A licensed, documented release lifts the dataset score toward "Enabling" rather than "Documentary" ([ARR review form](https://aclrollingreview.org/reviewform)).

In multilingual work, native data is decisive. Global MMLU showed that translated tests carry "cultural and regional assumptions" and shift model rankings ([Global MMLU](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.919/)). At ACL 2023, only about 64% of reviews of papers on non-English languages came from language-matched reviewers (as extracted, [ACL 2023 PC report](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.911.pdf)). The paper must therefore explain nói lái or xưng hô to non-speakers.

### The fallback ladder ends at a Student Research Workshop that probably skips the lottery

Papers not chosen for main are automatically considered for Findings ([ACL 2027 call](https://2027.aclweb.org/calls/main/)). In 2026, reviews came out March 10, commitment closed March 14 and notification followed April 4 ([ACL 2026 call](https://2026.aclweb.org/calls/main_conference_papers/)). By analogy, January-cycle meta-reviews should arrive around March 2027. A rejected paper keeps its reviews.

**ACL 2027 workshops** take ARR commitments in "April 2027" and direct submissions until April 25 ([joint workshop call](https://www.aclweb.org/portal/content/joint-call-workshops-proposals-2027)). **The ACL SRW** took ARR commitments until April 15 in 2026, eleven days after main notification ([ACL 2026 SRW](https://2026.aclweb.org/calls/student_research_workshop/)). NAACL 2027 workshops close commitments on March 12 ([joint call](https://2027.eacl.org/calls/workshops/)). Using them forfeits ACL, since committing to two venues is "Typically ... not permitted" ([ARR CFP](https://aclrollingreview.org/cfp)).

**A later ARR cycle** can take a revised paper. The exceptions are a meta-review score of 1.5, which skips the next cycle, and a score of 1, which bars resubmission. No cycle after January is dated. The 2025–2026 pattern implies a late-May cycle feeding EMNLP 2027, "tentatively" set for early November ([ARR dates](https://aclrollingreview.org/dates); [joint workshop call](https://www.aclweb.org/portal/content/joint-call-workshops-proposals-2027)).

**The SRW is the strongest safety net.** ACL 2025 required a first author who is "a current student (high school, graduate, or undergraduate)" ([ACL 2025 SRW](https://2025.aclweb.org/calls/student_research_workshop/)). ACL 2026 listed "highschool" among the students it serves ([ACL 2026 SRW](https://2026.aclweb.org/calls/student_research_workshop/)). EACL 2026 told high-school students "Yes" ([EACL 2026 FAQ](https://2026.eacl.org/calls/srw/faq/)) and accepted a benchmark by two of them ([2026.eacl-srw.26](https://aclanthology.org/2026.eacl-srw.26/)).

Nothing is confirmed for 2027. ACL's SRW page reads "Coming soon!" ([ACL 2027 SRW source](https://github.com/acl-org/acl-2027/blob/main/_pages/calls/student_research_workshop.md)). EACL 2027 names only "undergraduate or graduate" students, the wording EACL 2026 used before its FAQ said yes ([EACL 2027 SRW](https://2027.eacl.org/calls/srw/)). EACL 2026 also demanded dated proof of enrollment and limited AI help to "refinement tasks such as grammar correction, improving clarity, or rephrasing" ([EACL 2026 guidelines](https://2026.eacl.org/calls/srw/guidelines/)). The ACL 2026 SRW drew 402 submissions and reported a 34.7% acceptance rate. It allowed archival or non-archival papers and funded about $28,500 in student grants (as extracted, [SRW front matter](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-srw.0.pdf)).

ARR's FAQ says its service policy is "only for the 'main' ARR review cycles" ([ARR policy FAQ](https://aclrollingreview.org/sustainable-reviewing-2026)). EACL 2026's SRW waived ARR's "Reviewing Requirements" ([EACL 2026 guidelines](https://2026.eacl.org/calls/srw/guidelines/)). **A direct SRW submission therefore probably needs no contributor, though this is unconfirmed for ACL 2027.** The 2026 direct deadline was March 18 ([ACL 2026 SRW](https://2026.aclweb.org/calls/student_research_workshop/)).

We infer that the routes can be chained. The author could enter the lottery and, if the paper is not drawn, submit directly to the SRW, since a desk-rejected paper is no longer under ARR review. ARR has not said when the draw happens, so both sets of chairs should confirm this.

The SRW's free, optional mentorship served 114 papers in 2026. It aims "to improve the quality of writing and presentation of the student's work, not to critique the work itself" ([ACL 2025 SRW](https://2025.aclweb.org/calls/student_research_workshop/); [SRW front matter](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-srw.0.pdf)). Its deadlines (March 27, 2025; February 4, 2026) fell after the ARR deadline, so it helps only a direct submission.

The one SRW mentorship closing before January is EACL 2027's: apply by November 6, with feedback by December 5. Its high-school eligibility is unconfirmed. EACL also framed its 2026 edition as "not a parallel review channel" ([EACL 2027 SRW](https://2027.eacl.org/calls/srw/); [EACL 2026 FAQ](https://2026.eacl.org/calls/srw/faq/)).

No 2027 workshop list exists yet. Proposal decisions are due October 2, with no second call ([joint call](https://2027.eacl.org/calls/workshops/)). Past hosting makes C3NLP (at ACL in 2024 and 2026), GEM (2025 and 2026) and MeLLMs (2026) the likeliest ACL fits ([Anthology metadata](https://github.com/acl-org/acl-anthology/tree/master/data/xml)).

LoResMT, SIGTYP and VarDial rotate among conferences. If placed at EACL, COLING or NAACL, their deadlines (December 15 to February 5) would clash with the ARR submission ([joint call](https://2027.eacl.org/calls/workshops/)). MRL and WMT meet at EMNLP. SEALP had no 2026 edition ([Anthology SEALP](https://aclanthology.org/venues/sealp/)). VLSP (November 15, 2026, Ho Chi Minh City) runs yearly but does not count toward reviewer qualification ([VLSP 2026](https://vlsp.org.vn/vlsp2026)).

Direct workshop submissions fall outside the service rule. By our inference, non-archival slots allow early presentation without blocking a later archival paper; each call should be checked.

### Secondary-school first authors reached Findings only beside a senior researcher
